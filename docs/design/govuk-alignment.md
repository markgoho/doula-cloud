# The GOV.UK Design System, and what DoulaCloud takes from it

The [GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/) is the reference DoulaCloud reaches for whenever a screen has to **ask a person for something, or tell a person something went wrong**. This document says why, draws the line around what is taken, and holds the table that maps their catalog onto ours.

The decision itself is [ADR-0021](../adr/0021-govuk-is-the-reference-for-service-patterns.md). This document is the living half of it -- the ADR should not change, and the table below changes every time a screen is built.

## Why this system and not another

Most design systems publish components. GOV.UK publishes **decisions**, and it publishes the research that produced them. When their Dates pattern says a memorable date is three text inputs and never a date picker, that is not taste -- it is what happened when they watched people try. A four-month runway to launch cannot re-run that research, and does not have to.

The fit is unusually close, for three reasons:

1. **DoulaCloud is a service, not an app to browse.** A doula opens it to complete a task -- add a Client, send an Invoice, answer a message -- and leave. That is the shape of thing GOV.UK is built for.
2. **It is form-heavy and low-frequency.** A person who adds four Clients a month never builds fluency. Their patterns assume exactly that person.
3. **The users are people mid-crisis, some of the time.** A client filling in a birth plan after a loss, a doula doing admin between two births. GOV.UK's whole posture -- one question at a time, plain words, an obvious way out -- is designed for someone who has no attention to spare.

This is already how the product has been built. [ADR-0018](../adr/0018-templates-are-a-design-system-layer-with-two-named-exits.md)'s Template layer, the intake sequence drawn on the canvas in `e994dc79`, and the three-box date of birth drawn in `b7e8ab7` all came from there. This document makes that explicit rather than leaving it as a habit that only holds while someone remembers.

## What is adopted, and what is not

**Adopted: the decision.** What to ask, in what order, on how many pages. How to word a question, a hint, an error. Where the error summary sits and what its heading says. What happens to focus after a submit. Which of these things research says people get wrong.

**Adopted: the behavior.** A back link that really goes back. A `Change` link that returns to the question and comes back. A `Save and come back later` that saves a half-finished record. Three boxes for a memorable date.

**Not adopted: the markup.** No `govuk-*` classes, no Nunjucks, no `govuk-frontend` package. Our components are Svelte, our layout primitives are the custom elements of [ADR-0003](../adr/0003-css-layout-primitives-as-native-custom-elements.md), and the component tiers are Atomic Design per [ADR-0018](../adr/0018-templates-are-a-design-system-layer-with-two-named-exits.md).

**Not adopted: the look.** Transport, the yellow focus bar, the black-and-white chrome, the 1px-on-everything borders. The look is [the design brief](brief.md)'s -- Plum Dusk, evolved -- and the color, type and spacing truth is `app/src/lib/styles/tokens.css`. GOV.UK never overrules a token.

**Not adopted: the content.** Their patterns are written for UK government services. A National Insurance number, a UK postcode lookup, a sort code, a Welsh language toggle and a phase banner have no counterpart here. Those rows are marked N/A below with the reason, so nobody re-derives the same conclusion later.

The one-line version, which is also [the brief](brief.md)'s governing principle: **conventional in pattern and behavior, distinctive in execution.**

## Where GOV.UK has no pattern at all

A row is not automatically Open just because GOV.UK is silent on it -- silence is a prompt to walk a fixed order of sources, not a license to invent, per [ADR-0021's amendment](../adr/0021-govuk-is-the-reference-for-service-patterns.md#amendment-2026-09-01-what-to-do-when-govuk-has-no-pattern-at-all):

1. **GOV.UK Design System.**
2. **NHS.UK Design System and ONS Design System**, tied.
3. **Inclusive Components** (Heydon Pickering) and **Adrian Roselli's** writing, tied. [ADR-0057](../adr/0057-inclusive-components-stays-a-rank-three-source-and-a-control-that-acts-at-once-is-a-switch.md) confirms the book's rank, with two limits: the decision is taken and never the markup, and the book's browser facts are checked against today's support before use. For forms only (a screen that asks a person a question, checks the answer, or reports a refusal), **Form Design Patterns** (Adam Silver) is tied with them, by [ADR-0055](../adr/0055-form-design-patterns-is-a-rank-three-source-for-forms.md).
4. **Every Layout.**
5. **Original work, with a recorded reason.**

A row answered this way carries the status **Answered -- \<source\>** (e.g. `Answered -- ONS`, or `Answered -- Form Design Patterns` for a forms row), not Departed: nothing was overridden, because GOV.UK had no decision to override. See rule 5 below.

## Autocomplete: whose data it is

Every field that asks for a piece of personal information carries an `autocomplete` token, for two reasons. The browser can fill it, so a doula entering her own email or a Client entering her own address costs nothing rather than a re-type. And [WCAG 2.2's 1.3.5, Identify Input Purpose](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/identify-input-purpose.html), is level AA, so this is a standing accessibility obligation, not a nicety.

**The rule is the token names whose data it is.** A field about the person filling the form in gets the WHATWG token that names it -- `email`, `name`, `tel`. A field where a doula is entering **someone else's** information -- a Client, during intake -- gets `autocomplete="off"`. Offering the signed-in doula's own stored address for a Client's record is a data-entry hazard, and on a shared laptop it is a privacy one. This is [GOV.UK's own answer](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/addresses/): a form with two subjects keeps them apart rather than letting the browser guess which one it is filling in.

One field is not "whose data", but "which of two things is happening to it": on the two accept-invite screens, the email is either registering a new address (`email`) or naming an existing one (`username`), and the password is either being set (`new-password`) or recalled (`current-password`), depending on which of the two modes the person picked -- both derived from `mode`, not fixed markup. The plain login screens ask for the identifier only, which is always `username` even though the control is `type="email"`: that is the account identifier, not a new self-declaration, and matches how a browser's own credential manager reads a sign-in form.

`autocomplete="off"` is advisory -- browsers ignore it for passwords, and Chrome ignores it in other places too. Stating it is still worth doing, and a browser overriding it is not a defect this project can fix.

`TextInput` and `Textarea` both accept and forward the attribute, so a route never reaches around the atom to set it by hand.

## Page titles: format, ownership, and the words a link uses

Page titles are Service Manual guidance, not a Design System component -- there is no catalog row for `<title>`, which is why [#447](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/447)'s axe scan caught the whole application having none of them ([#487](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/487)) when this table's own walk could not have.

**Format.** `{Page} — {Service name}`, one em-dash, two segments -- GOV.UK's own `[Page] - [Service name] - GOV.UK` with the fixed third segment dropped, since nothing here plays that part. The Staff side's service name is `DoulaCloud`; the Client portal's is the Practice's own name, matching [#431](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/431)'s Practice-named chrome -- so `formatPageTitle` takes an optional `serviceName` and every portal Template/route that knows its Practice passes it. A page showing a genuinely refused form gets GOV.UK's `Error: ` prefix, matching `ErrorSummary` ([#467](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/467)); a data-load failure or a `+error.svelte` kind (404, 403, 503) does not, because its title already names the problem in words ("Page not found").

**Ownership.** One shared primitive, `#lib/components/PageTitle.svelte` (`formatPageTitle` in `#lib/pageTitle.ts`), not tiered under atoms/molecules/organisms/templates because it renders nothing visible -- it earns no `/style-guide` page. Every Template (`OverviewHub`, `RecordDetail`, `FormPage`, `QuestionPage`, `CheckAnswers`, `ErrorPage`, `EntryPage`, `ListPage`, `DocumentPage`) calls it internally with the same string handed to its `<h1>`/`<legend>`/`<label>`; every route with no Template calls it directly. Archetypes A, C, F and G all have a Template now (ADR-0018's 2026-09-02 and 2026-09-29 amendments), and every route of those shapes renders through one (#1576 moved the last five). The routes that still call it directly are the demo pages, and `clients/search`, which composes its own frame raw (ADR-0018's first named exit). The style guide sets one static title in its own layout. `pageTitle.usage.spec.ts` fails the pre-commit hook if a `+page.svelte` does neither, the same shape as `tokens.usage.spec.ts`. The Client portal's Practice name is preloaded through `portal/(authenticated)/engagements/[engagementId]/+layout.ts` rather than an onMount fetch, so the title (and the top bar) never flash from generic to Practice-named.

**Tracking.** The tab title says what the `<h1>` says, question by question on `QuestionPage` -- the title changes on every step of a journey, because that is 2.4.2's whole point. The style guide's ~70 demo pages get one static title (`Style guide — DoulaCloud`) from the shared `+layout.svelte`, not one per component.

**Link text should match the page it goes to -- with a carve-out GOV.UK itself draws.** A link whose sole visible text names a whole destination page (a nav item, a "Back to Clients" link) should read the same as that page's title once it is reached -- otherwise the destination surprises the person who followed it, and 2.4.4/2.4.9's own test (link purpose determinable from the link, or the link plus its immediate context) gets weaker with every mismatch. A spot survey while building #487 found this mostly already holds, and where it breaks down is instructive:

- **Nav items already comply.** `StaffTopBar`/`PortalTopBar`'s labels ("Clients", "Billing", "Your care") are the same strings now handed to those pages' `PageTitle`, because both were written from the same route inventory.
- **`DataTable`'s `rowHref` pattern already gets this right, structurally.** The linked cell's visible text is `column.accessor(row)` -- the row's own name, not a generic "View" -- so a Clients row already reads as "Jane Smith", which is what her `RecordDetail` page's own title will say. No separate disambiguating text is needed because the link text *is* the distinguishing content, for a sighted user and a screen-reader user navigating a links list alike.
- **`DataTable`'s `rowActions` column is a different category and this rule does not apply to it.** Staff's "Revoke"/"Edit membership" buttons perform an action in place; they do not navigate to a page with its own title, so "link text matches destination title" has nothing to check there. (They wanted per-row disambiguating text for a *different* reason -- a screen-reader user hearing "Revoke, Revoke, Revoke" out of row context -- which is a distinct accessible-naming concern from this one and is now fixed; see the row below.)
- **Where it broke down, and is now fixed:** `practices/[practiceId]/clients/[clientId]/+page.svelte`'s bare `label="Edit"` link carried no distinguishing text -- the one place the spot survey found the rule genuinely violated. It now carries the same GOV.UK fix `CheckAnswers`'s Change links already used: a sibling visually-hidden span naming the Client, joined by `aria-describedby`, so the announced name becomes "Edit, Jane Smith". [#513](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/513) walked every anchor in the application to confirm this was the one violation, not just a spot check.
- **CTA-style action links are a different case again, and this rule doesn't bite them.** "Manage staff", "Buy credits", "Payment settings", "Find or add a Client" and similar imperative links describe an action rather than purporting to name the destination page verbatim -- unlike a nav item or `DataTable`'s `rowHref`, whose whole visible text *is* a page or record's own name. A screen reader user still knows where each one goes from the phrase alone, so #513's audit did not treat a mismatch between "Manage staff" and the Staff page's own title as a violation. Same reasoning covers the Practice-name links (the switcher, the login/accept-invite/account pickers): the link text is the Practice's own name, and it lands on that Practice's Overview, whose top bar names the Practice -- the scope the link chose, not a surprise. The Overview's own `<h1>` is "Overview", the name of the nav item that opens it ([#2141](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/2141)); it was "Welcome to {name}" until then, a first-visit greeting shown on every visit that repeated the top bar.
- **The GOV.UK Back link is a named exception, not a violation.** `BackLink`'s default label is literally "Back" -- GOV.UK's own pattern, reused by `QuestionPage` and `CheckAnswers` -- and #513's audit found two portal routes (`engagements/[engagementId]/contract`, `.../birth-plan`) hand-rolling the identical thing with the bare `Link` atom instead of the extracted molecule; both now use `BackLink`.
- **Repeated per-row action buttons were a distinct, separately-scoped problem, now fixed.** `DataTable`'s `rowActions` and the Field Template editors' (now `PracticeQuestionsEditor`, #2024, and `ClientFieldTemplateEditor`) per-field "Move up"/"Move down"/"Remove"/"Archive" controls repeated the same bare word down a list -- the "Revoke, Revoke, Revoke" problem this table already flagged as out of #513's scope. #513 confirmed the category and filed the accessible-naming pass for it separately: [#515](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/515) added a `describedBy` prop to the `Button` atom, the same mechanism `Link` already had, and joined every repeated per-row Button found across the application -- Staff's "Edit membership"/"End sessions everywhere"/"Remove from practice"/"Show older changes"/"Revoke", both Field Template editors' "Move up"/"Move down"/"Remove"/"Archive", `OfferSection`'s "Withdraw" and `OfferInbox`'s "Accept"/"Decline" -- to a visually-hidden sibling naming the row, the same GOV.UK pattern `CheckAnswers`'s Change links and #513's Edit link fix already use.

#513 walked every anchor in the application -- nav, `DataTable` `rowHref`, the Practice pickers/switcher, all CTA links, the style guide's own component catalog (exempt the same way `pageTitle.usage.spec.ts` already treats it: one static title covers every demo page), and every `Link`/`BackLink`/`ErrorPage` usage. The one violation above is fixed; nothing else in the walk needed a change.

## Sign in, never log in

Decided on [#2008](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/2008), 2026-10-26. Every screen says **sign in** and **sign out**: the two sign-in screens' heading and button, the landing's doors ("Staff sign in", "Client portal sign in"), every way out of an error page, every "Continue to sign in" and "Sign in to get a new link", and every message that tells a person to sign in again. The app had said "Log in" for the act and "Sign out" for its end, so a person met both words for one thing.

The source is GOV.UK's style guide, whose [A to Z entry "sign in or log in"](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/a-to-z-style-guide/) says: "Use sign in rather than log in (verb) for calls-to-action where users enter their details to access a service." The Design System's patterns name neither word, so the style guide is the one GOV.UK source that decides it, and the brief's Voice (rule 6, a line one person says to another) agrees: "sign in" is what the drawings and the Client portal already say. `app/src/lib/copy.signIn.usage.spec.ts` fails the build on "log in", "log-in", "logging in" or "log out" in rendered copy, with `voice:ignore` and a reason on the line as the override.

Two things this does not change. The route paths `/login` and `/portal/login` stay: a path is not copy a person reads as a sentence, and moving it is a routing change, not a wording one. The same style guide entry also says not to use "login" as a noun; "Delete your login" on Your account is that noun, and renaming it is a decision about what the thing is called, left open on #2008's report rather than settled here.

## Where we depart on purpose

A departure is only legitimate if the reason is written down. These are the departures.

**A Staff member's name is two fields: First name and Last name.** GOV.UK prefers one full-name field, because a name does not always fit two parts, and it gives the two-field labels and tokens for the service that must split. We split for the reason the Client's name is split, below: the product addresses a person by her first name from the moment a screen knows it (the design brief's Voice rule, applied to Staff by [#663](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/663)), and one field cannot supply a first name reliably, because "Mary Anne Smith" has no safe split and the avatar used to guess its initials from white space. The labels are GOV.UK's own ("First name", "Last name"), the tokens are `given-name` and `family-name`, and each empty field has its own refusal in GOV.UK's wording ("Enter your first name", "Enter your last name"). The risk that stays is a person with one name only: she types it in First name and has to put something in Last name. Departed by [#1537](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1537), decided on [#1493](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1493).

**Names are split into given, family and preferred.** GOV.UK asks for a full name in one field, because splitting it breaks for people whose names do not fit the shape. We split because [ADR-0017](../adr/0017-twelve-columns-a-practice-defined-layer-and-an-engagement-that-is-asked-for.md) makes given name the one required column -- a Client can be saved with nothing else -- and because the product addresses a Client by her first name on every screen, which [#463](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/463) makes a copy rule. A single field cannot do either. **Preferred name** is what carries the risk their guidance is about: a person whose name is not what a document says gets a field for it, and the product uses that field.

**A name carried from the search is never split.** The search has one Name field, and a miss carries what was typed in it onto the name question ([#498](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/498)). GOV.UK says not to guess the structure of a name, so the name question does not guess which word is the given name and which is the family name. A value of one word carries into Given name. A value of two or more words -- white space of any kind between them -- carries into neither field, and the person types the name into the fields she means. The founder decided this on [#1716](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1716#issuecomment-5969001319); a single full-name field was out of its scope. `carriedGivenName` in `app/src/lib/intakeJourney.ts` is the rule, and the search's no-match copy says the name carries only when it does. A name of two or more words is still shown: the name question opens with `You searched for "Yar Pell". Type the name into the fields below.` above the fields, so the person does not have to remember what was typed one screen earlier -- recognition over recall, which the experience review reads a screen against. The line is read-only. It fills no field, is not split, and is not saved, so it sits apart from the "From your search, also saved with the name:" list. `searchedName`, beside `carriedGivenName`, is its rule; decided on [#1758](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1758#issuecomment-5975979586).

**No confirmation pages.** Their pattern gives a completed transaction its own page with a green panel. DoulaCloud announces outcomes in place with `Notice` and leaves the person where they were, because a doula sending an invoice is mid-task and has four more things to do, not finishing a once-a-year interaction with a government. This is a genuine trade -- the outcome is less emphatic -- and it is the right one for a tool someone works in all day. The `Panel` component follows the pattern out. Where a save has to leave the screen it was made on, the screen it lands on carries the `Notice` instead, once ([#1710](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1710)): the departure keeps an outcome on every route, not only on the ones that stay put.

**No task list, and no "complete multiple tasks".** Their task list is for a long application a person returns to over days. DoulaCloud's equivalent surface is the Practice landing page, which answers *what needs doing today* out of live data -- unsigned contracts, unpaid invoices, unanswered messages -- rather than tracking one person's progress through one submission. A task list would be a second, weaker answer to a question already answered.

**One pre-selected option on a question: *Who is this Visit for?*** Their [Select](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/select/) guidance draws the line explicitly — "If you use the component for settings, you can make an option pre-selected by default when users first see it. If you use the component for questions, you should not pre-select any of the options in case it influences users' answers." Asking who a Visit is for is a question, and the Add-a-Visit picker on the Engagement page opens on the signed-in person when she is on the Doula roster. The reason it departs: **the concern that rule protects against is a default influencing an answer the service does not know, and here the service already knows it.** `assignee` in `api/internal/visit` treats an absent assignee as the caller, which is exactly why a plain Doula is never shown this question at all — the API takes her write with no assignee on it. A Doula who also owns her Practice is asked only because she is allowed to read the roster, not because the answer is in any doubt, so she has to find her own name among fourteen to record work she just did herself. The pre-selection restores parity with a fact the session has already proven, rather than guessing at one an empty field would have collected. The residual risk their guidance names is real, because the same Owner also books her colleagues' Visits, which is why two things are part of the behavior and not decoration: her option is **first** in the list, and its label carries **"(you)"**, so the standing answer is legible as an answer and a deliberate change away from it is visible at a glance. Anyone the roster does not contain — an Owner or Admin holding no Doula role — still gets the placeholder and still has to choose. Departed by [#909](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/909).

**A second pre-selected option on a question: *What timezone does this Practice work in?*** The same [Select](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/select/) rule as the Visit picker above, departed on the signup form for a related but distinct reason. The screen opens on the zone the browser itself reports (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`). The concern the rule protects against is a default influencing an answer the service has no basis for, and here the service does have one: the person's own machine has already answered, so she is confirming a report rather than being nudged toward a guess, and the control is a plain select she changes in one press. Leaving it blank would make every founder answer a question her computer already knows, and the cost of a wrong answer is not cosmetic — the zone decides which calendar day a Visit falls on, so a founder who skims past an empty select has nothing to skim past, while one who skims past a pre-filled correct answer loses nothing. The residual risk is a browser reporting a zone the Practice does not keep its calendar in, which is why the answer is asked as a question with its consequence stated in the hint rather than inferred silently and never shown. Two things keep it honest: a reported zone outside the seven US options is **added to the list and shown by its own name**, so the control never renders blank against a value it does not carry, and a browser that reports nothing pre-selects nothing and the form refuses the submit until she chooses. The Practice settings screen that changes the zone later is a *setting*, not a question, so its pre-selection of the stored value is the rule applied rather than departed from. Departed by [#1166](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1166).

**A third pre-selected option on a question: *Who is the Doula?*** The same [Radios](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/radios/) and Select rule, departed on the Start work form ([ADR-0017](../adr/0017-twelve-columns-a-practice-defined-layer-and-an-engagement-that-is-asked-for.md), amended on [#1515](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1515)). The form asks which Doula is on the new Engagement, with **No Doula yet** as the last option; the list holds each Doula who can be attached without an Offer, which is each employee Doula and each Doula who holds Owner or Admin ([#1625](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1625)). Where the person at the form is the only Doula at the Practice, her option is selected when the form opens. The reason it departs is narrower than the Visit picker's: the service does not know she is the Doula, but the question has exactly two answers and one of them is true for nearly every Practice in that state, the solo doula for whom the persona rule is "wherever the app asks which Doula, the answer must default to her". An empty question would make every solo Owner answer it on every Engagement she ever starts. The risk the guidance names is real for one person, the agency Owner on her first day, before any Invitation has been accepted, who is the only Doula on the roster and may not be the Doula on this birth. Three things keep it honest: her option carries **"(you)"**, **No Doula yet** is on screen beside it and is one press, and the pre-selection stops the moment a second Doula joins the Practice, after which nothing is selected and the form refuses the submit until she chooses. A wrong answer is also cheap to undo: the Practice takes her off the Engagement, which ends the Attachment and deletes nothing. Decided on [#1515](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1515) and built by [#1596](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1596). The control is `RadioGroup` up to seven Doulas, so **No Doula yet** is on screen beside her own name. Past seven it is a select ([#2034](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/2034), see the Radios row), which lists her own name and **No Doula yet** first; with more than one Doula nothing is pre-selected, so the departure does not reach the select. Walked 2026-10-02 on the real page (`bun run dev:full`, a signed-in founding Owner) at 320px, with two rosters. **One Doula:** the question shows two options, *Anne-Marie Ochieng-Whitfield (you)* selected and **No Doula yet** below it; the form is submitted with no press on the question, and the new Engagement's Activity reads "Put on this Engagement as the Doula: Anne-Marie Ochieng-Whitfield" beside her own name as the person who did it. **Fourteen Doulas:** fifteen options in one column, the person at the form first, the rest in name order, **No Doula yet** last, nothing selected, and no sideways scroll (the document is 305px wide in a 320px window). A submit with no answer puts "Select who the Doula is, or select No Doula yet" in the error summary and against the question, and the summary link moves focus to the first option. Naming a colleague and submitting writes "Put on this Engagement as the Doula: Hana Kim" with the Owner as the person who did it. The walk found one defect that no spec had: the two longest names, *Bernadette Vandenberghe-Castellanos* and *Marguerite Throckmorton-Balasubramanian*, dropped below their radios, so each radio stood alone on a line with the name under it. It is fixed in `RadioGroup` (see the Radios row), and the fourteen-Doula roster was walked again after the fix: each long name now wraps beside its radio. The approval screen was walked at the same width: it shows *Doula: Marguerite Throckmorton-Balasubramanian* as a fact, and, after her Employment type was changed to contractor, **Approve and start the work** answered with the reason in the error summary and left both decisions on screen.

**A pre-filled answer: *Date received* on Record a payment.** GOV.UK's rule, which [ADR-0055](../adr/0055-form-design-patterns-is-a-rank-three-source-for-forms.md) makes the general one, is that a question opens with nothing chosen, and a pre-filled answer is a departure that records its reason. Recording a payment by hand on an Invoice (`InvoiceSection.svelte`) opens with *Date received* filled in with today, read in the Practice's own zone, and puts today back each time the form opens. The reason it departs: today is the most common answer, because a Staff member usually records a payment on the day it comes in, and the date is easy to change. Two things keep it honest: the form refuses a date after today, and the review step shows the date again before anything is recorded. The same form's *Method* goes the other way and opens with nothing chosen ([#1913](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1913)), because how a Client paid has no most common answer the service knows. Decided by the founder on 2026-10-24 on [#1923](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1923).

**No breadcrumbs.** [The brief](brief.md) settles a flat six-item nav. A flat structure has nothing to breadcrumb, and adding them would imply a hierarchy that is not there.

**A modal `Dialog`, confirming a destructive action in place.** GOV.UK has never published a Dialog/modal component -- their own backlog issue has been open since 2018 and a comment as recent as May 2026 is still asking the team to revisit it. Its load-bearing reason is progressive enhancement: their 2019 acceptance criteria required a modal to still let someone complete their task with JavaScript unavailable, because government services are built to survive that. That requirement does not bind here -- DoulaCloud is `ssr = false` and client-rendered end to end, so every screen already depends on JavaScript to render at all, not only this one; adopting their no-JS floor would mean rebuilding the whole app, not this component. Their separate, real caution is accessibility, and it does not transfer either: a 2023 finding on their own hand-rolled search-page modal showed VoiceOver and TalkBack ignore the `aria-modal` attribute and can swipe past it into the page behind. `molecules/Dialog.svelte` carries none of that risk -- it is the native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()`, which the browser makes the rest of the page `inert` for at the platform level, not by an ARIA hint a screen reader may or may not honor. Their own documented pattern for confirming a destructive action is in fact a separate yes/no page (HMRC's *add to a list*), and underneath it sits a preference this project knowingly overrides: prefer letting someone undo an action over asking them to confirm it first ("Never Use a Warning When You Mean Undo"), reserving a hard confirm for the genuinely irreversible. Two of the four actions `ConfirmDialog` guards -- ending a Staff member's sessions, revoking a pending Invitation -- are in fact reversible, and an undo-first design was weighed for them. [#473](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/473) keeps block-over-warn instead, for all four: a hard block with a deliberate, action-named override, the same house rule that already governed every other destructive action in this app before this ticket, so the four stay consistent with each other rather than splitting by reversibility. The `inert` this paragraph counts as a strength is also what settles *where a refused confirm gets reported*, and that half is the Error summary row above applied rather than departed from: a rejected `onConfirm` leaves the dialog open ([#804](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/804)), so anything rendered in the page behind it is under the `::backdrop` and cannot be focused, which is why `ConfirmDialog` carries its own `error` and renders it as a `Notice` inside the dialog. An `ErrorSummary` never goes in there, and that is a rule rather than a size judgment: its entries are fragment links, and HTML's fragment-focusing steps cannot focus an `inert` element, so every targeted entry would be the link to nowhere useful that row forbids -- and a second summary competing with the page's one is what [#467](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/467)'s `Notice`/`ErrorSummary` split exists to prevent. So the refusal's own shape picks the half: one that names no control is the dialog's own operation outcome and stays inside it, and one that names a control on the form behind is a refused form, so the dialog closes and the page's own summary takes it, with focus that lands because the page is no longer `inert`. Recorded on [#1082](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1082), whose Client-edit override confirm is the one call site whose refusal can carry either shape.

**Feedback opens over the screen, not on a page.** A person tells us about the screen they are on, so the screen stays where it is while they write: a drawer slides over part of it, and takes the width, as a modal, on a narrow screen. The outcome is a `Notice` in place, per the confirmation-page departure above. The trade: an unsent draft does not survive a reload ([#1502](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1502)).

**Three doors where GOV.UK has one start button: the signed-out landing at `/`.** GOV.UK's [Start using a service](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/start-using-a-service/) pattern has one start button, because it serves one service to one kind of reader. The app root serves three kinds of reader, and each has a different door: a doula or practice owner signs in to the Staff side, a Client signs in to the portal, and a new practice signs up. One start button would have to lead to a second page that asks which of the three she is, which is a page that only routes. So the three doors are on the first page, each one link with its name and one line that says who it is for. **Staff sign in** is the primary door, filled and with an arrow, because the marketing site is where a new person arrives ([ADR-0044](../adr/0044-the-founder-goes-where-doulas-already-gather-and-the-site-is-arrived-at.md)), so most people who reach `/` already have an account; ADR-0046 counts nothing about who visits, so this is a judgment and not a measurement. The other two are bordered doors, and their names look like every other link. The `<h1>` still names what the page is for, and the time-of-day greeting above it is a paragraph, not a heading. Departed by [#1645](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1645).

## Keeping this table honest

Five rules, so this does not become a document that was true once.

1. **A new screen that asks a person for something checks this table first.** If GOV.UK has a pattern for it, the burden is on departing, not on adopting.
2. **A departure gets a row here with its reason** on the same commit that departs. A departure with no recorded reason is a defect, and it is the only kind of GOV.UK finding this project treats as one. **A departure is knowingly overriding a GOV.UK decision that exists.** When GOV.UK has no decision to override, rule 5 applies instead -- the row is not a departure and must not be recorded as one.
3. **A row that says Open names a ticket.** No row says "someday".
4. **A row only says Aligned once somebody has seen it render**, at a desktop width and at the floor set by [ADR-0024](../adr/0024-layout-is-intrinsic-and-320px-is-a-conformance-commitment.md) -- 320px, and stated there rather than here so the two cannot drift apart -- and it carries the date that happened. Source is not evidence here. This table's first version was written by reading the code, which is the right way to audit a *decision* -- whether a date is three inputs, whether a page asks one thing -- and the wrong way to audit a *rendering*. Four rows failed the first walk ([#475](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/475), 2026-08-30) and none of them could have been caught any other way: the error message sat below its control, the six-column table scrolled the page sideways only at 320px, and two rows named a commit or a ticket that no longer said what the row claimed. `LabeledField`'s label did the same thing before them ([#425](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/425)). A row whose walk date is older than the component it names has not been checked. **And a style-guide entry that shows one of a component's states hides the rest**: two of these rows could not be walked at all until `/style-guide/error-page` stopped demoing only `notFound`, and the six-column table defect was invisible because the Data table entry demos two columns.
5. **A row GOV.UK is silent on names which source answered it.** Walk [the source-precedence order](#where-govuk-has-no-pattern-at-all) and record the winning source as `Answered -- <source>`, e.g. `Answered -- ONS`. This is not a departure: rule 2's burden of a recorded reason is for overriding a GOV.UK decision, and there is none to override here.

What this document deliberately does not do is track GOV.UK's own releases. Their system changes; ours does not have to follow. The table is checked when we build a screen, not when they ship a version.
