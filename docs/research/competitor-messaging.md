# How the tools a doula might choose instead talk about themselves

Research for GitHub issue #995 ("How the tools a doula might choose instead talk about themselves"), a child of the go-to-market map #991. The question: when a doula looks around for software, what does she read and hear? This is April Dunford's "competitive alternatives" input. It is not a feature matrix; the feature survey is `docs/research/doula-crm-table-stakes.md` on branch `research/doula-crm-table-stakes` (#173), and this file reuses its roster.

**Point-in-time capture, 2026-09-08.** Every quotation below was taken from the vendor's own home page, pricing page, or a first-party landing page on that date, by fetching the raw HTML and reading the headings and body text. Nothing is paraphrased inside quotation marks. Marketing copy changes without notice, so treat every line as dated to the capture. Social presence was read from each site's own footer links; where a public profile page returned a follower count to an anonymous request, that count is recorded with the same date. Instagram pages return a login wall to an anonymous fetch, so no Instagram counts appear here.

**Roster.** The nine tools #173 counted: Doulado, BirthFlow, BirthBase, PracticeLite (doula-specific); Practice Better, Jane (clinical); HoneyBook, Dubsado, Bonsai (generic). SimplePractice was surveyed but not counted in #173 and is covered here on the same terms, because a doula can choose it. Also covered: D-Tech, eDoula.biz, and Mobile Doula as doula-specific names that surfaced since; Agiled, a generic tool that now runs a doula landing page; and, as its own row, a spreadsheet and a group text. A web search on 2026-09-08 for doula practice-management software surfaced no doula-specific product that #173 had not already named; Practice (practice.do), which a search result listed with a doula page, returns 404 on its home page, its pricing page, and that doula page.

**Fields.** For each tool: the verbatim home-page headline; the sentence that says who it is for; the alternative it defines itself against; how it frames price; whether it speaks to a solo doula or an agency; the proof it shows; and the channels it is visibly present on.

## Bottom line

Every tool a doula might choose says the same three things: it keeps everything in one place, it gives her time back for the work, and she can start for free. Every one of them prices as a monthly subscription, most by the seat, and every pricing page says the plan grows with her. The doula-specific tools all speak to both the solo doula and the agency, with the agency as the higher tier; the generic tools speak to an independent business owner and sell a team as an add-on; the clinical tools speak to a clinic about patients.

Only BirthFlow names the competition. Its home page tells a doula to "Stop jumping between Dubsado, Google Notes, and your text history," and its compare page puts HoneyBook, Dubsado, and Doulado in a table with a row labeled "Built Specifically for Doulas." Everyone else defines itself against a pile of disconnected tools, a spreadsheet, or paperwork, without naming a product.

The claim no tool makes: none of them tells a doula how many doulas use it. The generic and clinical tools count small businesses and practitioners in the hundreds of thousands; the four doula-specific tools show no customer count at all, and the one doula count on any page is PracticeLite's "28+ Organizations signed up," which counts police crisis teams alongside private practices. Second, and closer to what Doula Cloud sells: no tool prices per client engagement. BirthFlow comes nearest with client-count bands and "Add Clients for $3/month," which is still a subscription.

## No software: a spreadsheet and a group text

The no-software alternative has no vendor and no page, so it cannot be quoted. It is filled in on the same seven terms so it sits in the comparison honestly; every line is inference from what the thing is, not a capture.

- **Headline:** none. It never had to be sold to her; it is what she already had open.
- **Who it is for:** everyone who has a phone and a Google account, which is every doula on day one.
- **Defines itself against:** nothing. It is the default that every tool above defines itself against. BirthFlow's "five different apps, three notebooks, and a mountain of text messages", BirthBase's "spreadsheets and generic CRMs", Bonsai's "no spreadsheets", and Agiled's "via text and email" are all descriptions of this row.
- **Price framing:** free, or already paid for. Venmo takes a percentage; a sheet and a group text cost nothing and never send a renewal email.
- **Solo or agency:** solo, completely. It breaks at the second doula: one sheet has one owner, a group text has no roles, and a backup doula gets a forwarded screenshot.
- **Proof:** ubiquity. Every doula knows another doula who runs on it, which is more social proof than any tool in this file shows for doulas specifically.
- **Channels:** none, and none needed. It is not marketed; it is inherited.

The consequence for positioning: a doula choosing Doula Cloud is not usually leaving a competitor, she is leaving this row, and the vendors know it, which is why the same three claims below appear on every page.

## The claims every tool makes, and the claim no tool makes

### Claims every tool makes

Counted across the eleven tools with a readable page (Doulado, BirthFlow, BirthBase, PracticeLite, D-Tech, Practice Better, Jane, SimplePractice, HoneyBook, Dubsado, Bonsai). A claim is counted only where the words are on the home or pricing page captured above.

1. **"One place."** Eleven of eleven. Doulado "all in one place"; BirthFlow "all-in-one platform"; BirthBase "all-in-1 solution"; PracticeLite "in one place"; D-Tech "all-in-one platform"; Practice Better "all-in-one practice management software"; Jane "one connected system"; SimplePractice "EHR and practice management software" with "Add-on features available to every plan"; HoneyBook "all in one place"; Dubsado "your whole business"; Bonsai "The unified platform". The phrase is so universal that BirthFlow uses it against the others: "the unique logistics that other "all-in-one" platforms ignore."
2. **"Time back for the work."** Eleven of eleven. BirthBase and PracticeLite use the same sentence, "spend more time with clients and less time on paperwork"; D-Tech "Spend less time on admin, more time with clients"; BirthFlow "You carry enough. Let us carry the rest."; Jane "reduce admin"; SimplePractice "Streamline admin tasks"; HoneyBook "more time to do what you love"; Dubsado "handle everything else"; Practice Better "streamline operations"; Bonsai "Streamline your client workflow"; Doulado's version is in its customer's mouth, "changed my ability to do the work I need to do".
3. **"Start free."** Ten of eleven say it in the hero or on the pricing page: Doulado "Start for Free"; BirthFlow "Start Your 7 Day Free Trial"; BirthBase "try out BirthBase for free - up to 3 clients"; PracticeLite "Start free"; Practice Better "Start free trial"; SimplePractice "Free 30-day trial"; HoneyBook "Start for free. No credit card required"; Dubsado "Start for free"; Bonsai "Try Bonsai for free"; D-Tech has no product to try. Jane is the exception: its calls to action are "Sign up" and "Book a demo", and a demo login is offered through its community.
4. **"Grows with you."** Seven of eleven use the words on the pricing page: PracticeLite "grows with your practice"; Practice Better "designed to grow with your practice"; HoneyBook "Plans that grow with your business"; Dubsado "plans that grow with you"; Bonsai "scales with you"; BirthFlow "your software grows with you"; Jane "as my team changes". The remaining four price in tiers that do the same thing without the sentence.
5. **A monthly subscription, by the seat.** Eleven of eleven price per month, and every tool that speaks to a team charges for the extra person: Doulado seats, BirthFlow "$40/mo" per doula, BirthBase "a small fee per doula", PracticeLite "$29/mo each", Practice Better "$50 per practitioner", Jane per practitioner who books, SimplePractice "$74/practitioner", HoneyBook by plan, Dubsado by user band, Bonsai per user. Client caps are the second dial: BirthBase 3 free, PracticeLite 5 free, Practice Better 10 on Starter, BirthFlow 15 to 150 by plan.

Two claims are near-universal among the doula-specific tools and absent from the rest: **"built by doulas"** (BirthFlow "Built for Doulas, By Doulas" and the founders' agency; BirthBase "Created in consultation with experienced doulas"; D-Tech "Founded in Detroit by visionary Doula and Advocate Camaria Wafer"; PracticeLite instead says "Built by a former SimplePractice engineer"; Doulado says nothing about who built it), and **HIPAA** as a word on the page (Doulado, BirthFlow, PracticeLite, and all three clinical tools; not BirthBase on any of its three pages captured, and none of HoneyBook, Dubsado, or Bonsai).

### The claim no tool makes

**No tool tells a doula how many doulas use it.** The generic and clinical tools count in the hundreds of thousands, but they count "small businesses", "creatives", "practitioners", and "clients", never doulas. The four doula-specific tools show no customer count at all: Doulado has four anonymous quotes, BirthFlow has its founders, BirthBase has one named doula and "doulas everywhere", and PracticeLite's "28+ Organizations signed up" counts a police crisis team. HoneyBook's doula page, the most polished doula page in the set, has no doula in it. A doula reading around on 2026-09-08 cannot find a single page that says "N doulas run their practice here."

Second, and the one that touches Doula Cloud's own pricing: **no tool prices per client engagement.** Every tool charges her for the month whether or not she has a birth in it. BirthFlow comes closest, with plans "Best for 15 Clients" and "Add Clients for $3/month", and BirthBase and PracticeLite gate their free tiers on client count, but every one of those is still a monthly seat. No page says "pay when you take a client, and not otherwise."

Two smaller absences, noted for the message work rather than the positioning: no tool makes any claim about the record it keeps after the engagement is over (who did what and when, or what the doula can take with her if she leaves), and no tool speaks to the family's side of the birth except as a portal to pay through. BirthFlow's "Birth Partner Portal" and HoneyBook's "Instill peace of mind and confidence in expectant mothers" are the only sentences in the set addressed, even sideways, to the client.

## Confidence and gaps

**Captured cleanly, first-party:** Doulado, BirthFlow (home, pricing, compare, story), BirthBase (home, pricing, agency), PracticeLite (home, doula page, pricing), D-Tech, Practice Better, Jane, HoneyBook (home, pricing, doula page), Dubsado, Bonsai, Agiled's doula page.

**Captured with a caveat:**

- **SimplePractice.** The static HTML has no hero heading; the headline row uses the page title, meta description, and banner text. A rendered browser would show a hero line that is not quoted here.
- **Dubsado pricing.** Only annual totals are in the table ("$335/yr", "$525/yr"). A monthly figure may exist behind a toggle that the static page does not expose.
- **Practice Better pricing.** The page carries two price sets, the plan cards ($35, $69, $155 monthly) and a comparison row ($25, $59, $89, $145, annual). Both are quoted where used; neither is the "real" price without the billing toggle.
- **HoneyBook Essentials.** Its monthly price did not extract from the static page; Starter and Premium did.
- **Promotions.** HoneyBook ("Get 40% off"), SimplePractice ("50% off for 3 months"), and Doulado ("2 Months Free") were all running offers on the capture date. The list prices are the durable numbers.

**Not captured:**

- **eDoula.biz** renders "Loading..." to a plain fetch; only its meta description is quotable.
- **Mobile Doula** (mobiledoula.com) timed out twice.
- **Instagram counts** for every tool. Instagram serves a login wall to an anonymous request, so presence is recorded from the vendor's footer link and nothing is claimed about follower counts or posting activity. The same is true of BirthBase's YouTube channel, which returned a page without a parseable subscriber count.
- **Facebook groups and doula conferences.** This file records where each vendor links from its own site. It does not record whether a vendor posts in doula Facebook groups, sponsors a DONA or CAPPA event, or is talked about on a doula podcast, because none of that is on a first-party page. That is the "where doulas gather" question, which belongs to its own ticket on #991, not this one.
- **Ontraport's doula page** was seen in search and not fetched.

**Method note.** Every quotation came from the raw HTML of the page named, fetched with curl and read through a heading and body-text extractor, then checked by hand. Third-party roundups that surfaced in search (agiled.app's "9 Best Client Management Software for Doulas", bloombirthstudio.com's "5 Best Doula Business Software Options for 2026") were used only to confirm that no new doula-specific product had appeared, and are not cited for any claim.
