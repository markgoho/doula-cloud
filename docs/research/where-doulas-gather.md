# Where doulas gather, online and in person, and what it costs to be there

Research for [#993](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/993) on the Go to market map ([#991](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/991)). The question: where do US birth and postpartum doulas gather, who is in each place (solo doulas, agency owners, or both), how many, whether a vendor may post or must pay, and what it costs. Every fact below was captured on **September 8, 2026** from the venue's own pages unless the row says otherwise. Where a venue's own evidence could not be reached, the row says **unverified** and names what blocked it; nothing is guessed.

## How to read this file

Each table carries the four lines the ticket asks for: who is there, size, vendor rule, cost. "Unverified" is a literal value, never a blank. Sizes are the venue's own numbers, and a number from a secondary source is marked as such. A "vendor rule" is what the venue says about a company posting, exhibiting, or advertising to its members.

Fetch limitations that shaped the evidence: Facebook groups return a login wall to every unauthenticated fetch, so no group's member count or rules could be read; Reddit returned HTTP 403 "Blocked" on every path tried (`www`, `old`, `api`, `.json`); Instagram and TikTok hashtag counts sit behind login; `icea.org` answers every request with HTTP 307 and no `Location` header, so ICEA's own pages were unreadable and its conference facts come from ICEA's own text as indexed by web search. Those four are the "unverified" clusters below. A logged-in pass with the founder's own accounts would close them in an hour.

## Summary: the five places most worth a founder's time before January

Applying the map's rule (only a place that can produce a paying Practice owner or solo doula earns a presence):

1. **DONA International Summit, October 26–27, 2026, online.** The one national gathering inside the window with a virtual exhibit hall, scheduled "Break and Visit Exhibitors" slots, and an attendee list handed only to exhibitors and sponsors. Exhibitor cost is unpublished; it comes from `conferencecoordinator@dona.org`. Non-member registration alone is $374 through the regular window.
2. **The Rochester Doula Cooperative and Beautiful Birth Choices.** The local market in two first-party lists: the Cooperative's directory shows 56 birth doulas, 35 postpartum doulas, and 15 birth-planning doulas; Beautiful Birth Choices runs the DONA-approved birth doula workshop and a postpartum doula training in Rochester, so it is where the county's new solo doulas come from. Membership terms and vendor rules for both are unverified; a conversation, not a fee, is the cost.
3. **The Doula Darcy ecosystem: podcast plus My Doula Village.** The only venue found whose host is a postpartum agency owner and whose membership runs a monthly office hour "specific to doula agency owners." Podcast guest spots are free; the membership is $97 a month or $970 a year and includes a private Facebook group of "over 100 doulas."
4. **ProDoula.** The business-first certifying body: $95 a year, a member page and public doula search on prodoula.com, a monthly newsletter that reports certifications by month (65 in February 2026), and a Beginner Business Training on Zoom on September 29, 2026. Its vendor or advertising terms are unverified.
5. **ICEA ELEVATE, October 16–18, 2026, Salt Lake City.** The only in-person national conference inside the window; sponsor and exhibitor packet not yet published at capture. Travel makes this the expensive option against a $2,500 pool.

Paid costs that are actually published, summed: DONA membership $100 + DONA Summit non-member registration $374 + ProDoula membership $95 + CAPPA membership $75 + one month of My Doula Village $97 = **$741**. Every exhibitor or advertising fee that matters (DONA Summit, ICEA, International Doula magazine, NBDA) is unpublished and must be requested by email; the channel plan should budget for them as unknowns, not zeros.
