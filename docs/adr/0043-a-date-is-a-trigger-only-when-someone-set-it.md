# A date is a trigger only when someone set it

Whether a message ever goes out because a date arrived was decided in this repo one mechanism at a time, and the answers point two ways. [ADR-0038](0038-overdue-is-derived-and-notifies-nobody.md) says every outbox is nudged by an act, a due date passing is not an act, and a passing due date notifies nobody; [ADR-0035](0035-connecting-stripe-is-system-noticed-except-when-there-is-no-account.md) refused a scheduled sweep over unconnected Stripe accounts on the same reasoning and gave a colleague an ask bounded to one a week; [ADR-0028](0028-the-shell-has-no-notification-bell.md) put unfinished work in a list and never in a badge; [ADR-0014](0014-the-waitlist-lives-outside-our-stack.md) gave the waitlist one confirmation email and one broadcast in January, and the teaser copy fixed on [#362](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/362) promises "I'll write to you once, when it opens." Yet [ADR-0031](0031-practice-deletion-is-a-thirty-day-window-then-the-erasure-cascade.md) fires a reminder at day 23 of a deletion window, enqueued at the Owner's own initiation, and `api/internal/billing/dormancy.go` lists a Practice whose unspent Credits nobody has touched for two years for a notice that New York's escheat statute makes necessary. Both are messages sent because a date arrived, and neither record called the other a contradiction, because nobody had named the line between them. [#1266](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1266), which asked what a Practice with untouched signup Credits ever hears, was closed as not planned on 2026-09-10 to wait for that line.

On 2026-09-15 the founder tested every one of those decisions against the twenty-nine books on the values track, in the second grilling session under [ADR-0041](0041-a-book-enters-the-repo-as-evidence-and-becomes-a-rule-only-through-a-decision.md), on [#1372](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1372), from the theme synthesis at [`docs/research/books/themes/the-clock.md`](../research/books/themes/the-clock.md). Twelve questions over two rounds; every recorded decision held, the line between the two clocks was named, and the questions the record left open were settled. He confirmed the positions record on that ticket. This ADR is the value behind the mechanism: why each position holds, in his reasons, with the evidence weighed and what was rejected. Page citations are as the read-backs in `docs/research/books/` give them, each under its own page rule. A book is evidence here and nowhere the reason.

**A message goes out on a date only when a person's own act, or the law, fixed that date in advance. Time passing with nobody acting is never a reason to write. What a Client hears and when is her Practice's to set, and the product carries it without ever composing it.**

## The decision

### A date fixed by an act is that act's consequence; elapsed silence is not a trigger

A message may go out on a date when a person's own act fixed that date, or when a statute did. A message may not go out because time passed with nobody acting. The founder's reason: the product carries what a person set and never decides on her behalf that silence needs answering, because who is written to, when, and in what words is the Practice's relationship, not the product's. This names the line the record already draws. The deletion reminder at day 23 is a consequence of the Owner's initiation on ADR-0031; the dormancy notice is a consequence of New York's APL 1315(1-b); the sweeps ADR-0038 and ADR-0035 refused were the product discovering silence and speaking first. The two Cloud Scheduler jobs in `terraform/scheduler.tf`, which drain the outboxes every five minutes and probe Practice Pages every fifteen, are not messages and sit outside the rule.

Weighed for: *Software as a Science*'s guardrail emails, which fire on events rather than on days elapsed since signup and change by whether the user is on or off track ([software-as-a-science](../research/books/software-as-a-science.md), ch. 6, PDF pp. 141–142); *Competing Against Luck*'s personal adviser, triggered by a red flag rather than by a date ([competing-against-luck](../research/books/competing-against-luck.md), ch. 3, PDF p. 60); *Forget the Funnel*'s warning that measuring engagement by logins turns a team into a nudger of customers who bought the product to stop logging in ([forget-the-funnel](../research/books/forget-the-funnel.md), ch. 7, PDF pp. 96–97).

Weighed against and rejected only where they would have the product discover silence: *Lean Marketing*'s automations triggered by behavior and elapsed time side by side, with no preference between them ([lean-marketing](../research/books/lean-marketing.md), Chapter 4, pp. 84–85), and *The 1-Page Marketing Plan*'s follow-up on preset timers beside prospect action ([the-1-page-marketing-plan](../research/books/the-1-page-marketing-plan.md), Chapter 5, p. 156). Dib's timers start from a prospect's own act, and the rule admits them.

### The waitlist hears once until January

The confirmation email is the last email a subscriber gets before the January opening broadcast. Nothing goes to the list between October and January. The founder's reason: the teaser's fixed copy says "I'll write to you once, when it opens," and the first thing Doula Cloud says to a stranger is the one it cannot take back; a weekly email from a product that is not open yet would teach her that Doula Cloud fills her inbox, which is the wrong first lesson. One consequence is accepted with it: the founding grant closes when the product opens, so the confirmation is the only notice of the grant a subscriber ever gets, and it has to carry the whole pilot offer and the way to say yes.

Weighed for: *This Is Marketing*'s rule that the promise made when permission was given fixes the frequency, kept until both sides agree to change it, whatever the campaign ([this-is-marketing](../research/books/this-is-marketing.md), Ch. 17, PDF pp. 153–154); *Entrepreneur Revolution*'s warning that a half-hearted newsletter annoys rather than delights ([entrepreneur-revolution](../research/books/entrepreneur-revolution.md), Chapter 5, PDF pp. 122–125); *The Voltage Effect*'s finding that continuous messaging goes stale ([the-voltage-effect](../research/books/the-voltage-effect.md), Chapter 6, PDF p. 140).

Weighed against and rejected: *Lean Marketing*'s floor of once a week, below which the list decays into unsubscribes and spam complaints (Chapter 12, pp. 199–201); *Marketing Made Simple*'s at least weekly for as long as it takes, so the seller is still there when the buyer is ready ([marketing-made-simple](../research/books/marketing-made-simple.md), ch. 8, pp. 131–132); *Start Small, Stay Small*'s relevant information every two to four weeks ([start-small-stay-small](../research/books/start-small-stay-small.md), Ch. 4, pp. 138–140); *Building a StoryBrand 2.0*'s drip campaign repeated month after month ([building-a-storybrand-2](../research/books/building-a-storybrand-2.md), ch. 15, PDF pp. 198–205). All four are written for a list that will be sold to for years; this list is at most 150 people over three months. ADR-0014's rejection of Kit as funnel automation stands: every email to the list is a broadcast a person chose to send because something happened.

### A Practice that starts nothing gets one message, on a date her signup fixed

A Practice that signs up, receives the three-Credit signup bonus, and has not started an Engagement fourteen days later gets one email, and nothing after. The founder's reason: her signup is the act and the date is its consequence, which is the deletion-reminder shape and admitted by the rule above; three free Credits were kept on [#1369](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1369) because an Engagement runs weeks and three is a solo doula's first quarter, and this is the one thing that tells her the trial is there. The message goes from the product's own identity under [ADR-0011](0011-notification-sending-identity-is-one-shared-domain.md), as a transactional message about an account she opened, so ADR-0014's promotional reasoning does not apply. It is skipped at send if she has started an Engagement by then, which is ADR-0031's recheck. It never goes to a Practice on a founding grant, which is spoken to by name. It points at the first useful act and never at the Billing screen. Because it is one message and not a sequence, there is no preference to turn it off, and [ADR-0029](0029-email-suppression-is-address-keyed-and-outbox-wide.md)'s suppression is the only thing that stops it. The build is [#1381](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1381), which refiles #1266 with all of that decided and the copy left to it.

Weighed for: *Start Marketing the Day You Start Coding*'s own case, where a trial with zero emails leaked the funnel and check-ins took installs from 25% to 75% ([start-marketing-the-day-you-start-coding](../research/books/start-marketing-the-day-you-start-coding.md), "Small Startup Acquisition", pp. 213, 224, 228); *Company of One*'s one automated email a week after purchase, credited with doubling sharing ([company-of-one](../research/books/company-of-one.md), ch. 10, PDF pp. 119–120); *Forget the Funnel*'s onboarding email series at SparkToro, credited with doubling free-to-paid two months later (ch. 8, PDF pp. 110–111); *Competing Against Luck*'s finding that the first institution to speak to a prospect usually closes (ch. 3, PDF p. 59).

Accepted as a limit, not a reason to move: *Forget the Funnel*'s nudger warning (ch. 7, PDF pp. 96–97) and *Entrepreneur Revolution*'s mediocre send that annoys (Chapter 5, PDF pp. 122–125), which is the limit the count of one respects. Rejected: a sequence, whether *The SaaS Playbook*'s onboarding emails over the first sixty days ([the-saas-playbook](../research/books/the-saas-playbook.md), 80/20 SaaS Metrics, pp. 151, 159, 161) or *Building a StoryBrand 2.0*'s drip (ch. 15, PDF pp. 198–205), because a sequence that sells is promotional mail on the transactional domain and the product does not chase.

### A paying Practice is never chased; the product may list, and a person acts

A paying Practice never hears from the product because time passed. The product may list quiet Practices on elapsed time, read-only and internal, for a person to read and act on by hand, and that list is a build ticket only when the pilot gives it Practices to list. There is no digest. The two-year dormancy notice is sent by a person from its list, as `dormancy.go`'s read-only design already says. The founder's reason is the rule's: the product carries what a person set and never decides on her behalf that silence needs answering. The dormancy handler is the shape already on trunk: software flags, a person judges, and the person's own act is what sends anything.

Weighed for: *Zero to One*'s PayPal fraud system, which stopped working while it decided alone and worked once a person made the final judgment on what the software flagged ([zero-to-one](../research/books/zero-to-one.md), ch. 12, PDF p. 110); *Software as a Science*'s record that calling a few dozen red accounts by hand surfaces people who had been meaning to cancel (ch. 7, PDF p. 163); *The Mom Test*'s MTV client, who got a Friday report nobody read ([the-mom-test](../research/books/the-mom-test.md), ch. 2, pp. 34–36), which is what a digest becomes.

Weighed against and rejected: *Software as a Science*'s trigger emails on a usage threshold, reaching an account before the customer knows anything is wrong (ch. 7, PDF pp. 150, 158, 161); *The 1-Page Marketing Plan*'s reactivation campaign to lapsed customers under "We Miss You" (Chapter 8, pp. 249–250); *Make*'s weekly grouped digest as the model for push ([make-the-bootstrappers-handbook](../research/books/make-the-bootstrappers-handbook.md), Launch, p. 80), a consumer-app case; *The Best Interface Is No Interface*'s system that reaches out on a person's inferred pattern before being asked ([the-best-interface-is-no-interface](../research/books/the-best-interface-is-no-interface.md), ch. 15, pp. 258, 260–261), where the side held is the same book's automation that earned trust over a long, careful track record (ch. 18, pp. 278–280).

### A Practice may schedule a Client reminder as her own act; the product never composes one

A Practice may schedule any Client-facing reminder, an invoice, a Visit, a due date, as her own act, in her own words, and the product carries it on the date she set. Nothing is sent by default, there is no template, and nothing is built before launch. The founder's reason: the Practice is the actor and the date is her act's consequence; who chases a Client, how soon and in what words is her relationship, which is ADR-0038's own reason, and the loss-journey check there applies to the copy she writes, which is why the product offers none. ADR-0038 stands as written: its mechanism and its reason are unchanged, this position gives the seam it named a rule for who may open it, and a Client-facing word for lateness is still its own ticket with its own loss-journey check.

Weighed against and rejected as a product default: *The 1-Page Marketing Plan*'s worked checklist chasing unpaid invoices at 7 to 13, 14 to 27 and 27-plus days (Chapter 7, pp. 229–230) and its reminders by post, email or SMS called fully automatable for products whose usefulness expires (Chapter 8, pp. 246–248). No book on the track answers ADR-0038's loss-journey reason, and it outweighs all of them.

### The January opening is two emails, and a third only on a real event

The January opening is two emails: the opening broadcast, and a founder note a week later to the whole list saying what the pilot Practices did with the product and how to start. A third goes out only if something real happens. There is no countdown and no manufactured deadline, and the pilot terms are not reopened to give an email one. The founder's reason: "when it opens" is an event with a beginning and an end, so two dated emails keep the teaser's promise in spirit; the founding grant closes when January opens and [ADR-0042](0042-a-practice-pays-per-engagement-and-doula-cloud-is-paid-when-the-doula-is.md) promised no time-limited price, so there is nothing for a third email to close on. The dates are [#1003](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1003)'s calendar.

Weighed for: *The Voltage Effect*'s finding that one-time incentives are easier to scale than continuous messaging (Chapter 6, PDF p. 140); *Entrepreneur Revolution*'s rule never to send a half-hearted newsletter, because a mediocre attempt at delight annoys rather than delights (Chapter 5, PDF pp. 122–125).

Weighed against and rejected: *Start Small, Stay Small*'s Ideal Launch, an email a week before promising a 48-hour price, another on launch day, and a final 12-hour reminder (Ch. 4, pp. 143–144); *Marketing Made Simple*'s six sales emails days apart closing on a limited-time offer, because a deadline forces action (ch. 9, pp. 147–151); *Building a StoryBrand 2.0*'s countdown timers at the commitment stage (ch. 15, PDF pp. 186–187). Each depends on a deadline the record does not have.

### Every send is measured by the act it asked for

Every send is measured by the first-party act it asked for: an Engagement started after the fourteen-day message, a yes or a reply after the confirmation, a signup after the January opening. Unsubscribes and complaints are the only negative signal. Buttondown's open counts are not read as a measure of anything. The founder's reason: a message nobody read cannot have done the one thing it was sent to do. This is [ADR-0030](0030-no-third-party-open-or-click-tracking-in-notification-email.md)'s rule, which the founder applies to a platform ADR-0030 does not govern.

Weighed for: ADR-0030; *This Is Marketing*'s test of how many people would reach out or complain if the next send did not go out (Ch. 17, PDF pp. 153–154), which measures an act. Weighed against and rejected: *Building a StoryBrand 2.0*'s deleted email that still accomplished the goal, beside an industry-standard open rate of 20 percent (ch. 15, PDF p. 204); *Marketing Made Simple*'s unopened mail that still brands because a name seen while swiping to delete registers (ch. 8, p. 132).

## What was considered instead

**A sweep that finds silence and writes.** Rejected because the product would be deciding on a Practice's behalf that her silence needs answering; ADR-0038 and ADR-0035 refused the mechanism, and this ADR records the value behind the refusal.

**A cadence to the waitlist before January.** Rejected because the teaser promised one email, and a promise made to a stranger is kept as written.

**Nothing, ever, to a Practice that starts nothing.** Put beside one message and not taken: the signup fixed a date, and one message on that date is the product doing what her act set, not chasing her.

**A sequence to a Practice that starts nothing.** Rejected because a sequence that sells is promotional mail on the transactional domain, and because the product does not chase.

**Health-index outreach, reactivation campaigns, and a digest.** Rejected because each has the product speak first on elapsed time; the list the product may keep gives a person the same facts and leaves the act to her.

**Automatic invoice chasing.** Rejected because it is a decision about a Practice's relationship with a woman in her third trimester, which is hers; the Practice may schedule her own reminder instead.

**A dated launch sequence against a deadline, and reopening the pilot terms to create one.** Rejected because the grant closes when the product opens, no price is time-limited, and a published term is not changed for an email.

**Open rates as a measure.** Rejected because an open measures image loading, not reading, and never the act the message asked for.

## Consequences

- `docs/manifesto.md` gains one line for this ADR, and nothing else.
- ADR-0038, ADR-0035, ADR-0031, ADR-0028, ADR-0014, ADR-0030 and ADR-0029 are unchanged. Each keeps its mechanism; this ADR carries the value that explains why the two clocks on trunk are not a contradiction.
- #1381 builds the fourteen-day message with its audience, timing, count, identity and opt-out already decided; its copy is fixed on that ticket first.
- [#361](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/361) writes the confirmation email as the only notice of the founding grant a subscriber ever gets; #1003 records "during" as one email and "after" as two dated emails with a third only on an event, and names an act as every item's signal.
- A Practice-scheduled Client reminder and the internal list of quiet Practices are fog on the billing map, not tickets, until the pilot gives either a reason to be built.
- Where a later grilling session on another theme touches when a message goes out, this ADR is the record it tests against, and a change lands as a superseding ADR, not an edit here.
