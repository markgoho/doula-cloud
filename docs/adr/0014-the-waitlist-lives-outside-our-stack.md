# The pre-launch waitlist lives outside our stack, in Buttondown

The pre-launch teaser site ([map #359](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/359)) collects an email address and a first name, sends one confirmation email carrying the pilot-group offer, and broadcasts once in January 2027. That list is kept in **Buttondown**, a third-party newsletter platform, and not in our Postgres behind the BFF — even though we already run a BFF, already have Mailgun wired with an outbox and a bounce webhook, and could obviously write the row ourselves.

This is deliberate, and the reason a reader will not guess is **sending-domain reputation**.

`mg.doula.cloud` is our transactional sending domain. It carries Client portal invites, which [ADR-0010](0010-notification-email-delivery-is-an-outbox-not-in-request.md) established are the *only* path a Client's invite token has, under the one shared sending identity of [ADR-0011](0011-notification-sending-identity-is-one-shared-domain.md). A cold marketing broadcast to a list of strangers earns spam complaints; Mailgun's own guidance is to keep promotional mail off the subdomain that carries transactional mail, precisely so those complaints cannot reach the reputation the transactional program depends on. So "roll our own on Mailgun" is not the reuse it appears to be — it means standing up a *second* verified sending domain with fresh DNS, SPF, DKIM and DMARC, zero sending history, and a warm-up period before the January blast.

**The build list underneath that is the rest of the cost.** A public POST endpoint would be the first unauthenticated write surface in `api/` — every other route is behind `staffauth.Middleware` or is signature-verified. `docs/api-design.md` §6 specifies rate limiting but no package implements it, so the limiter would be built from nothing. Firebase Hosting offers no WAF, so bot defence means a honeypot plus a third-party challenge widget — a vendor dependency arriving anyway. The `hugo` Hosting target has no rewrites at all today, so `/api/**` would need a scoped one. Then a table, a goose migration, tests against the 100% coverage gate, double opt-in end to end (token, confirm endpoint, confirm page), unsubscribe end to end (token, endpoint, page, honoured at send time — CAN-SPAM is not optional), and a batched January send. Three to five sessions, against a teaser targeted at 1 September 2026.

**What we give up is small and was checked, not assumed.** Nothing inside the product reads this list: the pilot-group onboarding in January is a handful of agencies spoken to by name, not a batch job over a CSV. So the audit-trail and own-the-row arguments — normally decisive here — buy nothing for a record nothing queries. The list exports as CSV, so the choice is reversible for the cost of an afternoon.

## Considered options

- **A public endpoint on the Go BFF, with our own table.** Rejected on the sending-domain problem plus the build list above. It is the right shape for a record the product reads; this record is not that.
- **Mailgun mailing lists.** Rejected on capability, not on principle. Mailgun gives a list, a `subscribed` flag, send-to-the-list-address broadcast, and a working `%mailing_list_unsubscribe_url%` — but **no hosted signup form and no double opt-in flow**; its own best-practice pages tell you to build those. It also does not solve the sending-domain problem, since it is the same domain.
- **Kit (formerly ConvertKit).** The strongest rival: free to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited sends, against Buttondown's 100-subscriber free tier and roughly $9/mo beyond it. Kit lost on fit and on operator judgment after the cost case was made in full, not on facts — it is automation software for funnels, and this is one person sending two emails to a small list. Recorded here so the next reader does not re-propose it believing it was never weighed.
- **beehiiv** (2,500 free), **EmailOctopus** (2,500 contacts / 10,000 emails a month free), **MailerLite** (250 free, and already familiar to the operator). All viable, all between Kit and Buttondown on every axis, none better fitted.
- **Drip.** Rejected: $39/mo with no free tier, and the store integrations and revenue attribution that justify the price need an ecommerce store we do not have.
- **A client-side create-only Firestore write.** Rejected: refusal would live only in security rules, sending would still be unsolved, and the waitlist would sit in a store nothing else in the product uses.

## Consequences

- **Double opt-in is the junk defence, and it lives at Buttondown's boundary, not ours.** Buttondown requires it by default: an address that is never confirmed never joins the list. This is a stronger bot and typo filter than a rate limiter on an endpoint we would have written, and it costs nothing to operate.
- **The confirmation email is load-bearing and is a paid feature.** Buttondown gates confirmation- and welcome-email customisation behind `custom_transactional_emails`, so the free tier cannot carry the pilot-group offer. Buttondown is therefore a paid dependency from the first signup. Exactly which tier unlocks it was not verifiable from public pages and is pinned down by a task ticket before any email is written. **If that tier costs materially more than about $29/mo, this decision reopens.**
- **CAN-SPAM's postal address is still ours to answer.** No platform supplies a real address; see [#363](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/363).
- **Choosing a platform category is not settled here.** Whether DoulaCloud eventually needs an email *marketing* platform (segments, sequences, funnels) rather than a *newsletter* platform belongs to the distribution effort, which is its own map. Every candidate exports CSV, so this decision forecloses nothing there.
