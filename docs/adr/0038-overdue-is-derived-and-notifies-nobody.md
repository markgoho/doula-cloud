# Overdue is derived from a stored due date, and notifies nobody

Before this decision an Invoice had nothing to be late against. The Stripe rail sent `days_until_due=30` when it raised the bill, purely because Stripe's API rejects `collection_method=send_invoice` without either that or a `due_date`; the resulting date lived on Stripe's object and never came home to our row. A by-hand Invoice ([#271](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/271)) never reached Stripe at all, so it had no due date anywhere, in any system. A Practice with sixty Clients had no screen answering "who owes me, and for how long", which is the question an office manager opens the app to ask. Decided on [#768](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/768).

**Every Invoice carries its own due date, on both rails.** `invoices.due_at`, written at the moment the Invoice is raised. Deliberately not `due_date`: in this domain a due date is the *pregnancy's* (ADR-0015, on `engagements`), and the two must never share a word. The Stripe rail computes the instant once and sends Stripe that exact `due_date` rather than a day count, so the date printed on the Client's hosted invoice and the date the Practice's book ages against are one date rather than two independently derived ones that can differ by a day with neither being wrong.

**The overdue state is derived, never stored.** `status = 'open' AND due_at < now()`, evaluated by Postgres at read, with the day count derived where it is displayed. Stripe emits no event when a `send_invoice` due date passes — verified against the Sandbox on a test clock advanced a month past a `$50,000` Invoice's due date, which produced `invoice.finalized` and `invoice.updated` and no overdue event of any kind. Nothing in this repo fires on time either: every outbox is nudged by an act through `tasknudge`, and a due date passing is not an act. A stored flag would therefore need a new scheduled sweep to keep it true, and would be wrong between sweeps. The comparison is true at every instant and costs nothing to keep true. `invoice_status` gains no value for the same reason: an overdue Invoice is an `open` one.

**Payment terms are a per-Practice setting, defaulting to 30 days.** `practice_payment_terms`, one row per Practice, shaped after `practice_rates` ([#966](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/966)) — with one difference. There, a missing row means a rate genuinely nobody has set; here it means **30 days**, a real default, because every Invoice must have a due date on both rails and absence has to resolve to a number. 30 is what the Stripe rail already applied, so adopting it changes no existing Practice's behavior. **A day of payment terms is 24 hours, counted from the instant the Invoice was raised** — a duration, never a calendar day in the Practice's timezone, the database's, or anyone else's. That is the same count Stripe's `days_until_due` makes: measured on the Sandbox on 2026-10-02, `days_until_due=30` returned a `due_date` exactly 2,592,000 seconds (30 × 86,400) after `created`, across the 25-hour day of 2026-11-01. So "changes no existing Practice's behavior" holds to the second, and the due date stays what the rest of this decision needs it to be: an instant compared against an instant. ADR-0036's calendar day does not reach here; its subject is a `date` column, and `due_at` is not one. Stated on [#1626](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1626), after the count turned out to be inherited from the database session's timezone rather than written down. An Invoice keeps the terms it was billed under: changing the setting never moves a date already written. There is no per-Invoice override — whether one is needed turns on a business fact still open on [#979](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/979).

**A passing due date notifies nobody.** The Practice reads aging on its own Invoice list, which is where an office manager already goes to ask the question. Automatic chasing is deliberately not built: it would need a time-based sweep, and — more to the point — who chases a Client, how soon, and in what words is a decision about a Practice's relationship with a woman in her third trimester, not a default a product should pick on her Practice's behalf. The seam exists when it is wanted: `00008_messaging.sql` already anticipates an "Invoice-due" notification type reusing the notification outbox, and the site-build verify job is the precedent for a Cloud Scheduler sweep.

**The Client sees no new word.** Overdue is a Practice-side aging fact and does not reach the portal. `open` keeps the fixed Client label GLOSSARY.md gives it — "Not yet paid", decided on [#981](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/981) — unchanged. Two reasons. ADR-0005 draws the line: the register never invents a word for a fact the model could not hold, and until #768 the model held no lateness at all. And #981's own loss-journey check binds hardest here — "Overdue", in red, on a bill read by a woman whose pregnancy ended in loss, is the product shaming her for a payment her Practice has not even asked her about. If a Client-facing word is ever wanted, it is its own ticket with its own loss-journey check.

## Amended 2026-10-25: the due date is read as a day in the Practice's timezone

[#1965](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1965) changes the overdue boundary and keeps everything else above. A person reads a due date as a calendar day, not an instant: the #1958 walk showed an Invoice due `2026-11-24T00:00Z` printed as Nov 23 in New York, and under `due_at < now()` it was overdue from 7pm on that same Nov 23, a day its reader still thought she had to pay in. The day shown and the moment it became overdue disagreed by a day.

**The due day is the calendar day `due_at` falls on in the Practice's timezone, and an Invoice is overdue from the start of the day after it there.** ADR-0036's calendar day now reaches here: a due date is a day, and a day only exists somewhere. Not the reader's zone, for the reasons ADR-0036 gives, and not UTC's. `due_at` stays the stored instant, and a day of payment terms stays 24 hours from the instant the Invoice was raised: only the reading of the result changes. Stripe is still sent that exact instant as its `due_date`.

**Still derived, still never stored.** The BFF reads the instant once per request through `clock.Now(ctx)`, takes the start of that day in the Practice's zone (`overdueFrom` in `api/internal/payments/invoice_days.go`), and Postgres compares `due_at` against it: `status = 'open' AND due_at < <start of the Practice's today>`. That is the same test as "the due day is before today", and it stays an instant comparison on the stored column. The narrowing, the totals and each row's day count all use the one boundary, so they cannot disagree. The comparison no longer uses Postgres's `now()`, so a simulated clock moves it through `clock.Now`, the seam #773 made for that purpose.

**The BFF counts the days late.** The Practice-wide list's rows carry `dueOn` (`YYYY-MM-DD`) and `daysOverdue`, a count of whole Practice days. The app no longer computes lateness on the reader's own clock: if it did, a reader in another zone could see an Invoice called late that the figure above it does not count. The cost is the one the totals already had: a tab left open overnight shows the count from when the page loaded.

**Still notifies nobody.** No scheduled job and no reminder email reads `due_at`; this amendment adds none.

**A known gap: Stripe's hosted invoice.** Stripe prints its `due_date` in a zone of its own choice. Near midnight, the day on the Client's hosted invoice and the Practice's `dueOn` can be a day apart. "One date rather than two" above still holds for the instant, but not always for the printed day. Nothing here changes the instant to close that gap.

It was renumbered on [#1053](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1053), because [#1025](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/pull/1025) took 0033 twenty-three minutes earlier for `0033-staff-login-deletion-is-immediate-and-redacts-the-person.md` and one number cannot name two decisions. Every citation was repointed here except one: `api/db/migrations/00109_connect_nudge_outbox.sql` says "nothing in this repo fires on a clock (ADR-0033)" and means this decision, but that migration has already run and `api/db/migrations/guardrail_test.go` records why an applied migration must not change. `app/src/lib/adrNumbers.usage.spec.ts` now fails the build on a repeated ADR number, so the next one is caught in CI rather than by a reader.
