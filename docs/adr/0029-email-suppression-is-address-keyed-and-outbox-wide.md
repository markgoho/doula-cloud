# Email suppression is address-keyed and outbox-wide, not an Engagement preference

[#732](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/732) (map [#347](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/347)) needed to decide what happens after a Mailgun complaint or hard bounce on a Notification email — a question research on [#731](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/blob/research/731-can-spam-mailgun-suppression/docs/research/can-spam-transactional-exemption-and-mailgun-suppression.md) reopened by finding all six email-sending outboxes share one Mailgun domain (ADR-0011), and that Mailgun already refuses a suppressed send server-side regardless of anything Doula Cloud builds.

**Suppression is a new table keyed on the address, not a `notification_preferences` channel.** `notification_preferences` ([#303](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/303)) is keyed on `identity_uid` + `engagement_id`: a Client's own per-Engagement choice. Suppression is not a choice; it is Mailgun's reaction to what happened, and it must exist before any `identity_uid` does — the portal invite and the Staff invitation both send pre-account. `notification_channel` stays a one-value enum (`'push'`); nothing optional exists yet for an `'email'` value to gate.

**Both `complained` and a genuine fresh hard bounce write a suppression row.** Mailgun's webhook `event-data.reason` distinguishes the two: `suppress-bounce` / `suppress-complaint` / `suppress-unsubscribe` mean the address was already suppressed and this send was never attempted (`delivery-status.code: 605`, no SMTP session); anything else on a `permanent`-severity `failed` event is a real SMTP-level rejection happening for the first time. Recording the real-bounce case adds no suppression risk beyond what Mailgun's own bounce list already causes automatically on the next attempt — it only gets Doula Cloud's own visibility ahead of that, instead of discovering it the next time a `suppress-bounce` reason comes back.

**Every outbox gets a pre-send guard, not just a webhook.** Today only `portal_invite_outbox` has a webhook at all ([#340](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/340)); without the guard, the others would keep retrying a known-dead address through a full backoff cycle before dead-lettering it, and produce no record that anything unusual happened. `staff_invite_outbox` is covered identically to the rest — ADR-0011 already put every one of them on the same domain and the same reputation, so there is no voice-based reason to track it differently.

**Amended by [#733](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/733), which built it: the guard is one `mail.Sender` decorator at send time, not a `NOT EXISTS` clause in each claim query.** Two things changed once the work started. First, the count: this decision said six outboxes, and there are eleven mail kinds, because `internal/offer`, `internal/engagementrequest`, `internal/authmail` (two) and `internal/mfarecoverymail` also send on the shared domain. Eleven hand-written claim guards is eleven chances to miss one, and every one of the eleven already reaches Mailgun through the single `mail.Sender` `main` constructs — wrapping that once covers all of them and covers a twelfth kind nobody has written yet. Second, the shape: a row excluded at claim time stays `pending` forever and records nothing, which is exactly the "no record that anything unusual happened" this decision argues against. Refused at send time it returns `mail.ErrSuppressed`, and `outbox.Worker.MarkFailed` dead-letters the row on the spot with the reason on it, skipping ADR-0010's backoff — waiting cannot un-suppress an address. A multi-recipient kind skips a suppressed address and still mails the others, and dead-letters only when every recipient was suppressed: one Practice owner who complained must not cost the others a Notification they never objected to.

**Bounce-caused suppression is Staff-clearable; complaint-caused suppression is not.** A bounce is often a stale or mistyped address; clearing it once Staff fixes the record costs nothing. A complaint means a recipient marked Doula Cloud's mail as spam on the one domain every Practice and every email type shares — re-sending risks a second complaint against that same shared reputation, not just against the one address. Clearing must call Mailgun's own `DELETE /v3/{domain}/bounces|complaints/{address}`, not just the local row, or the address stays refused server-side regardless of what Doula Cloud's own table says.

**Consequence:** a spam complaint against one Practice's portal invite permanently blocks that address on every other Practice's mail too, forever, by design — the cost of ADR-0011's shared domain, accepted there and inherited here rather than reopened.
