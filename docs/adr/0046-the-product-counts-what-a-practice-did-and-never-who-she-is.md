# The product counts what a Practice did, and never who she is

What Doula Cloud counts was decided one mechanism at a time. [ADR-0016](0016-teaser-analytics-are-cookieless-and-the-channel-rides-on-the-form.md) counts the teaser's visitors with Pirsch, cookieless, under legitimate interest with no banner, carries a subscriber's channel on four hidden UTM fields that are never joined to analytics, and reads channel counts as a floor, never a total; its amendment on [#368](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/368) rules out the card test, because card and audience confound, and makes `utm_content` a label, never a verdict. [ADR-0030](0030-no-third-party-open-or-click-tracking-in-notification-email.md) keeps open and click tracking out of every email in either voice. [ADR-0043](0043-a-date-is-a-trigger-only-when-someone-set-it.md) P7 measures every send by the act it asked for and reads Buttondown's open counts as a measure of nothing. [ADR-0044](0044-the-founder-goes-where-doulas-already-gather-and-the-site-is-arrived-at.md) P8 holds the launch targets, 150 confirmed subscribers and 5 pilot Practices, as numbers to be wrong about, and P6 puts no user count on the site. [ADR-0045](0045-a-doula-is-heard-before-she-is-chosen.md) P6 keeps three counts of "heard from" apart and calls only a Practice who bought a Credit a customer. [ADR-0022](0022-one-activity-log-with-a-subject-and-three-kinds-of-actor.md) makes the Activity log the Practice's own append-only record, read through the same gate as its subject. The `staff.last_active_at` column exists because 2 NYCRR 125.1 accepts a verifiable login as the contact that stops New York's three-year dormancy clock, and the dormancy sweep reads it. The pilot terms on [#444](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/444) ask nothing of a Practice, no feedback obligation and no data terms. Those records hold the mechanisms. None of them said whether the January site or the signed-in app counts anything, what stands in for churn on a model with nothing to renew, what the founder reads and how often, how a Practice who starts in January is attributed to a channel, whether any named person's use is read, or whether any surface ever runs a split test.

On 2026-09-18 the founder answered those questions against the twenty-nine books on the values track, in the fifth grilling session under [ADR-0041](0041-a-book-enters-the-repo-as-evidence-and-becomes-a-rule-only-through-a-decision.md), on [#1375](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1375), from the theme synthesis at [`docs/research/books/themes/what-gets-measured.md`](../research/books/themes/what-gets-measured.md). Nine questions over two rounds. Once the founder took a position short of the recommendation put to him, and the record says so. He confirmed the positions record on that ticket. This ADR is the value behind those records: why each position holds, in his reasons, with the evidence weighed and what was rejected. Page citations are as the read-backs in `docs/research/books/` give them, each under its own page rule. A book is evidence here and nowhere the reason. The repository is public: this ADR names the columns of the founder's monthly page and never a figure on it.

**The product counts what a Practice did, and never who she is. A Credit bought and a Credit spent are the two acts the product reads; a visitor is a number on the marketing site and nobody inside the app; the founder reads a page once a month and no figure on it is a goal.**

## The decision

### The January site counts visitors, and the app counts nothing about anyone

The January marketing site counts visitors with Pirsch on the teaser's terms: one more site on the account ADR-0016 already uses, cookieless, under legitimate interest with no banner, with the same line on `/privacy`. The signed-in app carries no third-party script of any kind, analytics or otherwise, and no first-party usage counter; it holds Client PHI under the Google Cloud BAA, and Pirsch offers a DPA, not a BAA. What the app knows about how a Practice uses it is two queries against the Credit ledger, the next position's. Cloud Run's request logs and error rates stay and are read as operations: they measure the service, not people. The founder's reason: the January site "is the same as the teaser site, just with more content", so the denominator ADR-0016 bought for the teaser, whether a disappointing month is a page that failed to persuade or a page nobody reached, is the same question in January; and the app needs "something very simple just pulled from queries run against the DB to track two metrics which matter the most".

Weighed for: ADR-0016, extended to the site it was written beside; _Design for Cognitive Bias_'s close-to-inverse relationship between how easy a metric is to measure and its value ([design-for-cognitive-bias](../research/books/design-for-cognitive-bias.md), ch. 3, pp. 48–49); _The Best Interface Is No Interface_'s argument that engagement measured as screen time rewards the wrong thing ([the-best-interface-is-no-interface](../research/books/the-best-interface-is-no-interface.md), ch. 5, p. 132); _Forget the Funnel_'s FeedOtter, where measuring engagement by daily logins would push the team to nudge customers back into a product they bought to avoid ([forget-the-funnel](../research/books/forget-the-funnel.md), ch. 7, PDF pp. 96–97); _Lean Marketing_'s record that the consumer and government backlash removed targeting, tracking and attribution capabilities from digital advertising ([lean-marketing](../research/books/lean-marketing.md), Chapter 13, pp. 223–225); _Make_'s own flag that Hotjar's session streaming is useful though its ethics might be questionable ([make-the-bootstrappers-handbook](../research/books/make-the-bootstrappers-handbook.md), Launch, pp. 80–81). Rejected: _Make_'s Google Analytics as good enough and its four tools (Launch, pp. 80–81), because the app is not a page and a Practice's use of it is not a visit; _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s metrics toolset built before selling and its dedicated data analytics chief on the senior management team ([the-startup-owners-manual](../research/books/the-startup-owners-manual.md), Web/Mobile, Ch. 8, PDF pp. 515–526), because there is nobody to hire before January and hiring is the eighth theme's; _Software as a Science_'s session recordings, click streams and customer health index ([software-as-a-science](../research/books/software-as-a-science.md), ch. 6, PDF pp. 139–141; ch. 7, PDF p. 159), because each reads a named person, which the fifth position refuses.

### Two counts from the ledger stand in for churn

Nothing renews and nothing cancels, so what stands in for churn is two counts read from the Credit ledger: Credits bought and Credits spent, one row per Practice per month, with the two totals beneath and bought-again read off the rows as the Practices with a second purchase lot. A `purchase` row is a lot with a quantity, so Credits bought is the sum; a `consumption` row is one Engagement started, so Credits spent and Engagements started are the same number. Credits spent is the leading act and the only one that moves before spring, because the founding grant is sized to last most of the pilot and a second purchase from the agency may not exist before then; bought-again is the kept signal, the nearest thing a model with nothing to renew has to a renewal, and an act she took rather than a silence. No elapsed-silence figure: "holds Credits and started nothing in N days" is the shape ADR-0043 rejected as a trigger, and it reads a solo doula's ordinary gap between births as a loss. No lifetime value before there is a tenure to average. No cohort by signup month before launch: with five Practices every row is its own cohort, and the cohort view is the first thing to add when a month's signups outnumber the page. The read is an internal endpoint in the shape of `DormantPracticesHandler`, behind the same guard, called by hand; it is [#1394](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1394). The founder's reason: these are "the two metrics which matter the most", "credits purchased and credits used", one row per Practice.

Weighed for: _Competing Against Luck_'s Little Hire, the moment of use that most data misses while it records the purchase ([competing-against-luck](../research/books/competing-against-luck.md), ch. 5, PDF pp. 92–93); _Forget the Funnel_'s measure aligned with the value bought, and its Value Realization as a threshold of customer actions (ch. 7, PDF pp. 96–97, 101–102), the value bought here being a birth run through the product; _The Mom Test_'s rule that a free thing counts as commitment only in proportion to what it costs the person ([the-mom-test](../research/books/the-mom-test.md), ch. 5, p. 70), with [ADR-0042](0042-a-practice-pays-per-engagement-and-doula-cloud-is-paid-when-the-doula-is.md)'s finding that a spent Credit costs a real family; _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s reservation of monthly recurring revenue, churn rate and lifetime value for subscription or recurring models ([the-1-page-marketing-plan](../research/books/the-1-page-marketing-plan.md), Chapter 8, pp. 254–255). Deferred until a month's signups outnumber the page: _The Lean Startup_'s cohort analysis as the gold standard, each cohort an independent report card, with gross totals drawing a hockey stick over a flat paying rate ([the-lean-startup](../research/books/the-lean-startup.md), Ch. 7 Measure, PDF pp. 121–123, 127–128); _The Voltage Effect_'s reading per region rather than as a national daily average ([the-voltage-effect](../research/books/the-voltage-effect.md), Chapter 7, PDF pp. 166–167). Rejected: _The SaaS Playbook_'s monthly recurring revenue and churn ([the-saas-playbook](../research/books/the-saas-playbook.md), 80/20 SaaS Metrics, pp. 144–145) and _Software as a Science_'s four numbers with the monthly churn rate as the largest lever (ch. 1, PDF pp. 30–31; ch. 2, PDF pp. 45–46), because nothing renews and nothing cancels ([#45](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/45); the pilot terms); _Lean Marketing_'s lifetime value from profit and average tenure (Chapter 15, pp. 254–256), because there is no tenure yet to average; _Competing Against Luck_'s SNHU statistic, whether graduates would do it again (ch. 9, PDF p. 187), because it is a would-you question and [#994](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/994) bans the form; _This Is Marketing_'s how many people would reach out or complain if the next send did not go out ([this-is-marketing](../research/books/this-is-marketing.md), Ch. 17, PDF p. 154), held as a question the founder may put to himself and never as a count on the page, because it is a guess about an act nobody took.

### The founder reads a page once a month, and no figure on it is a goal

The founder reads a page once a month, by hand, holding the three counts of ADR-0045 P6, the two counts above, the month's revenue, the month's cost and the pool's remainder, and nothing else. The counts come from the internal read and the revenue and cost from the bank. The business is judged by minimum viable profit, the month the business is in the black. Below fifty in the denominator every figure on the page is written as a count with its denominator beside it, never as a rate. The page is a private spreadsheet in the Doula Cloud Google account, one row per month, with these columns and nothing else on it. No complaint count, since `hello@` reaches a person who reads every complaint, and a count of them is Goldratt's ticket-volume measure. No weekly or daily board, because nothing on it can change in a week at this scale and a board read daily becomes a goal. No figure on the page is a target: the launch targets stay predictions to be wrong about (ADR-0044 P8). The founder's reason: "looks good", and, of the spreadsheet, it "would probably keep build cost down as well".

Weighed for: _Company of One_'s minimum viable profit as what determines sustainability rather than customer count, measured growth or gross revenue ([company-of-one](../research/books/company-of-one.md), ch. 11, PDF p. 127), and its exist strategy against quarterly increases or an exit (Epilogue, PDF p. 167); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s traffic, revenue and expenses computed monthly ([start-small-stay-small](../research/books/start-small-stay-small.md), Ch. 7, pp. 197–198); _The Lean Startup_'s three A's, a report that is actionable, accessible and auditable by hand against real customers (Ch. 7 Measure, PDF pp. 140–144); _Lean Marketing_'s dashboard of the handful that matter, expanded only when troubleshooting (Chapter 15, p. 259), and its warning that more fiction gets written in spreadsheets than in books (Chapter 15, pp. 257–259); _Forget the Funnel_'s Goldratt, that measurement determines behavior, with a support team measured on ticket volume replying with boilerplate (ch. 7, PDF pp. 95–96); _Design for Cognitive Bias_'s Goodhart's Law and its rule that whatever is incentivized gets gamed (ch. 3, pp. 41, 49); _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s systems, not goals ([the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant](../research/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant.md), Saving Yourself, pp. 183, 188), held as the rule that no figure on the page is a target; _Thinking in Bets_' single result that supports very little ([thinking-in-bets](../research/books/thinking-in-bets.md), ch. 1, PDF p. 34) and _Zero to One_'s statistics that do not work at a sample size of one ([zero-to-one](../research/books/zero-to-one.md), ch. 6, PDF pp. 46–47), as the reason for counts over rates. Held, from _The Voltage Effect_: the average hides the answer and the positive impact of the last dollar spent is the number (Chapter 7, PDF pp. 155–156, 161–162), which binds the day a channel is paid for and binds nothing while the pool buys presence only (ADR-0044 P4).

Rejected: _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s company dashboard with customer satisfaction rate and complaint count as candidates and incentives tied to metrics (Chapter 8, pp. 255–256), because a complaint is read and not counted and there is nobody to incentivize; _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s Weekly LAPS Dashboard of leads, appointments, presentations and sales ([entrepreneur-revolution](../research/books/entrepreneur-revolution.md), Chapter 4, PDF pp. 102–103), because the pilot offer is made by hand to a Practice who asks (ADR-0044 P1) and there is no pipeline to stage; _Software as a Science_'s daily dashboard (ch. 7, PDF p. 162) and its Growth Ceiling (ch. 1, PDF pp. 30–31), because the ceiling needs a churn rate; _Zero to One_'s customer lifetime value against customer acquisition cost (ch. 11, PDF p. 99) and _Lean Marketing_'s customer acquisition cost, return on ad spend and return on investment (Chapter 15, pp. 256–257), because the pool buys presence only and there is no acquisition cost to divide; _This Is Marketing_'s refusal to measure brand marketing (Ch. 15, PDF pp. 139–140) and _The Almanack_'s refusal of self-measurement (Saving Yourself, pp. 183, 188), as far as either would forbid the page, because a page read monthly and chased by nobody is not the measurement mania they warn against; a redacted file in the repository, because it records a page that says nothing; a page in the founder's own notes, because it is a record one person can read.

### A Practice is attributed by the tag on her signup, and asked in conversation

A Practice who starts in January is attributed to a channel the way ADR-0016 attributes a teaser subscriber: four hidden UTM fields on the signup form, set once, landing on the Practice as her source and never joined to analytics. The form asks no question about how she heard of Doula Cloud. The founder asks, in the past tense, whenever he is already talking with a Practice, and writes what she said in `docs/research/pilot-agency-interviews.md` beside the call it came from, which is where ADR-0045 already puts a doula's words. The tag is the floor, her words are the first touch, and neither is read as the other. The build is [#1393](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1393), and it lands before the January site opens, because a Practice created before the field exists is untagged forever. The founder's reason: "sure"; the position rests on the records named below and was taken as put.

Weighed for: ADR-0016's channel on the form and channel counts as a floor; [#1003](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1003)'s rule that an act on a `utm_source` is every channel item's signal; _Start Marketing the Day You Start Coding_'s ask customers how they found you, with last-click analytics under-crediting the first touch ([start-marketing-the-day-you-start-coding](../research/books/start-marketing-the-day-you-start-coding.md), "Four Things I Learned", pp. 150–152); _The Four Steps to the Epiphany_'s customers asked how they hear about new companies and products ([the-four-steps-to-the-epiphany](../research/books/the-four-steps-to-the-epiphany.md), Chapter 3, pp. 163–165); _The Mom Test_'s Buffer, where the conversations with everyone who signed up, not the conversion rate, were what convinced (ch. 6, p. 78); _Company of One_'s word of mouth that happens in a coffee shop or an untrackable private message (ch. 10, PDF p. 118); ADR-0045's notes file as where a doula's words go. Rejected: an optional "how did you hear about Doula Cloud" field on the signup form, because the form has one job (ADR-0044 P5) and a skippable field yields a floor no better than the tag; no mechanism at all, because the tag costs one hidden line and the door is one-way. Answered rather than rejected: _Landing Page Hot Tips_'s platform-specific coupon codes ([landing-page-hot-tips](../research/books/landing-page-hot-tips.md), Tip #15, PDF p. 42), since UTM at venue granularity is the same mechanism.

### No named person's use is read as a measure

Doula Cloud reads no named person's use as a measure of anything: no Staff login, no feature, no page, no Client act. The Activity log stays the Practice's own record, read through the same gate as its subject (ADR-0022), and Doula Cloud does not read it as analytics. `staff.last_active_at` exists because 2 NYCRR 125.1 accepts a verifiable login as the contact that stops New York's dormancy clock; it is read by the dormancy sweep and carried in the Practice's own data export, which is her record and not a measure, and by nothing else; this ADR writes that down. The one per-Practice moment that is named is First Value: an Engagement started on a real Client, which is the value she bought and already ADR-0045 P2's proof, read from the ledger and never from a login. The founder's reason, in his words: "no named person's use".

Weighed for: _Forget the Funnel_'s rejection of the login count and its value-aligned measure, with First Value set lower than Value Realization (ch. 7, PDF pp. 96–97, 101–102); _This Is Marketing_'s permission asset measured only by who would complain if the next send did not go out (Ch. 17, PDF p. 154); _Company of One_'s referral pitch that goes only to customers who rated their enjoyment above a seven (ch. 10, PDF p. 120); _Design for Cognitive Bias_'s clicks that are easy to measure and may say nothing (ch. 3, pp. 48–49); _The Best Interface Is No Interface_'s case against screen time (ch. 5, p. 132); _Competing Against Luck_'s active data that shouts while the passive data of the customer's struggle has no voice (ch. 8, PDF pp. 163–166). Rejected: _The Lean Startup_'s split tests and cohorts that follow individuals through registration, activation and retention (Ch. 7 Measure, PDF pp. 138–139), because the product's value moment is in the ledger and needs no person followed; _Software as a Science_'s Customer Health Index, red, yellow and green on logins, started from a guess it calls a SWAG (ch. 7, PDF pp. 152–159), because a health index on five Practices is a guess about people the founder can telephone; _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s cohort analysis of a sales funnel (Web/Mobile, Ch. 9, PDF pp. 564–578), because there is no funnel, the ask is "start" and the pilot offer is made by hand.

### No split test runs on any surface

No split test runs on any surface: the app is a no until the founder decides otherwise, and the marketing site is a no until decided otherwise. Changes on the site are chosen on the merits, as #368 put it for the teaser, and tested, when tested, the way _Copyhackers_ falls back to, in front of five to ten doulas, which is a call and not a counter. The recommendation put to the founder, that the app holds at never, was not taken as written: he holds the app at no and keeps the decision his own. The founder's reason, in his words: "no split test now, TBD for future"; the "app is a no (for now until I decide otherwise), marketing site is no for now until we decide otherwise".

Weighed for: #368's finding that two cards in two venues confound card with audience and a sequenced test confounds card with time; _Zero to One_'s local maximum reached by small changes to what exists, against designing by focus-group feedback (ch. 6, PDF pp. 59–60); _The Voltage Effect_'s caution as the fallback where three or four replications are not feasible, and its rule that the person with the idea should not be its only tester (Chapter 1, PDF pp. 38, 44); _Copyhackers: Uplift_'s user testing with five to ten people like the target audience when an A/B test is not available ([copyhackers-uplift](../research/books/copyhackers-uplift.md), Section 5, pp. 49–50). Rejected: _The Lean Startup_'s rule that a user story is not done until a split test shows changed behavior, with the unvalidated feature removed (Ch. 7 Measure, PDF pp. 136–137), because a doula's tool does not rearrange itself between two births; _Lean Marketing_'s always-be-testing as a durable practice for paid advertising (Chapter 13, pp. 225–227), because there is no paid advertising (ADR-0044 P4); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s price test by changing the price (Ch. 3, pp. 100–101), because ADR-0042 holds $20 through the pilot.

### What the record had already settled, held as recorded

Open rates are not read as a measure of anything and every send is measured by the act it asked for (ADR-0043 P7): _Building a StoryBrand 2.0_'s 20 percent industry standard and its deleted email that still accomplished the goal ([building-a-storybrand-2](../research/books/building-a-storybrand-2.md), ch. 15, PDF p. 204), _Marketing Made Simple_'s open rate per email ([marketing-made-simple](../research/books/marketing-made-simple.md), ch. 10, pp. 165–166), _Start Small, Stay Small_'s clicks and opens with subject lines tested (Ch. 4, pp. 141–143), _Company of One_'s 26 percent and 70.5 percent (ch. 8, PDF p. 102) and _This Is Marketing_'s Yoyodyne open rates over 70 percent (Ch. 17, PDF p. 152) are rejected on it. No email carries open or click tracking (ADR-0030).

No rating scale, no Net Promoter Score and no "would you do it again" is asked: #994 bans the would-you form and ADR-0045 P3 rewrote [#243](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/243) to the past tense, so _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s Net Promoter Score (Chapter 8, pp. 258–259), _Software as a Science_'s NPS in a mature health index and its hit above a 50 percent price rise (ch. 7, PDF p. 159; ch. 10, PDF p. 224), _Company of One_'s rating above a seven (ch. 10, PDF p. 120) and the SNHU statistic are rejected, with _The Mom Test_'s one-to-five scale as the formality it argues against (ch. 4, pp. 57–59) in agreement.

The site carries no user count and no pilot count (ADR-0044 P6), against _Building a StoryBrand 2.0_'s statistics as a way to show competency, modeled on 125,000 users (ch. 6, PDF p. 99). The pilot asks nothing of a Practice, no feedback obligation and no data terms (#444). The targets are predictions to be wrong about and never goals (ADR-0044 P8), which is _Thinking in Bets_' rule that a forecast is not shown wrong by the likelier future failing to arrive and that a guessed number is stated as a guess (ch. 1, PDF pp. 32–34; ch. 6, PDF p. 166) and _The Lean Startup_'s quantitative prediction made before the test (Ch. 7 Measure, PDF pp. 117–119) held together.

Held: _The Voltage Effect_'s answer to the Make-A-Wish Foundation, that some things worth doing transcend monetization and conventional measurement (Chapter 7, PDF p. 167), and _Competing Against Luck_'s Scott Cook, that there is no automatic way to measure the hours an accountant did not spend (ch. 7, PDF p. 148); the page does not claim to be the whole of what the product does, and nothing measures the birth a doula attended with the product out of the room. On the evidence, as ADR-0045 recorded for its theme: no book's uncited figure is the reason for any position above, not _Start Marketing the Day You Start Coding_'s mailing list converting up to ten times website traffic ("Nine Levels", pp. 18–21), not _Building a StoryBrand 2.0_'s 20 percent, not _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s lead-to-sale rate under 5 percent (Chapter 4, PDF pp. 103–104); every count in the books is its author's own practice, weighed as experience.

## What was considered instead

**Pirsch, or a first-party counter, inside the app.** Rejected because a Practice's use of the app is not a visit, the app holds PHI under a BAA that Pirsch does not offer, and the two acts that matter are already in the ledger.

**Nothing on the January site either.** Rejected because the denominator ADR-0016 bought is the same question in January, and the site is the teaser with more content.

**"Holds Credits and started nothing in N days."** Rejected because it is the elapsed-silence shape ADR-0043 rejected and reads an ordinary gap between births as a loss.

**A lifetime value, or cohorts by signup month, now.** Deferred: there is no tenure to average, and a cohort with one member is the Practice's name.

**A weekly or daily board, or LTV against CAC, or the Growth Ceiling.** Rejected because nothing changes in a week at this scale, the pool buys presence with no acquisition cost to divide, and the ceiling needs a churn rate.

**A "how did you hear" field on the signup form.** Rejected because the form has one job and a skippable field yields a floor no better than the tag.

**A health index, or cohorts through login, first Engagement and purchase.** Rejected because five Practices are people the founder can telephone, and the value moment is in the ledger.

**The app holds at never on split tests.** Put to the founder and not taken as written: the app is a no until he decides otherwise.

**Ries's rule that a feature is not done until a test shows changed behavior.** Rejected because a doula's tool does not rearrange itself between two births.

Below fifty in the denominator, a count with its denominator beside it, never a rate.
