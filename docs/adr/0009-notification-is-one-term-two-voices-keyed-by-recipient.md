# Notification is one term across channels; voice is keyed by recipient, not sender

Doula Cloud is about to send its first outbound email — a Client portal invite, replacing today's hand-pasted token link (map [#213](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/213)) — and needed a name for what it's sending. "Notification" was already in use for the browser Web Push mechanism (`app/src/lib/push.ts`'s `notificationFor()`, the browser's own `Notification`/`NotificationOptions` types, `service-worker.ts`'s `notificationclick`), and `api/internal/push/push.go` already anticipated this moment: its `FakePusher` comment says it is "deliberately generic... so a future non-Message notification type can reuse it without a new interface." Rather than coin a second word to dodge the collision, **Notification becomes the umbrella term for any push-delivered alert to a person, regardless of channel.** Browser push and email are both content-free alerts that point at the product rather than carry it — the same discipline [ADR-0002](0002-message-transport-push-triggered-fetch.md) already established for Message's push layer — so they are one concept with two delivery mechanisms, not two concepts. Channel is a clause inside the Notification entry, the same way "delivered via push-triggered fetch" is a clause inside Message's entry rather than a term of its own.

**A Notification has exactly two voices, and the voice is fixed by who receives it, never by what it's about.** A **Platform Notification** is Doula Cloud speaking as itself, to a Staff member or Owner — its own customer. A **Practice Notification** is Doula Cloud speaking as the Practice, to that Practice's Client. This is exhaustive and mutually exclusive: every Notification is one or the other. It is a different axis from *who technically sends it* (the Go BFF vs. Identity Platform) — that's the map's sender axis, decided per Notification type on later tickets, not here.

**The content rule is one rule for both voices**, mirroring ADR-0002's existing no-content push rule: no Client name, no Engagement detail, nothing identifying in subject or body. It carries exactly one exception — a Practice Notification may name its own Practice, in the `From` and/or subject, because without that it cannot be sent at all; there is no other Practice-facing content it may show. Whether naming the Practice is safe under HIPAA for a given tenant is a separate question, settled on the sending-identity ticket ([#216](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/216)) using the compliance research already done ([#214](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/214)); this ADR states the content rule assuming a nameable Practice, and #216 states the conditions under which that assumption holds.

**This extends [ADR-0005](0005-one-context-client-register-at-the-ui-edge.md).** ADR-0005 made the Client register "binding on the client portal and advisory everywhere else," because the portal was the only Client-facing surface at the time it was written. A Practice Notification is a second one. The register's justification — a Client should never meet a domain word she hasn't already met as her own word — applies to an email subject exactly as it applies to a portal heading, so the register's binding scope widens to **every Client-facing surface**, Practice Notification wording included. ADR-0005 carries a forward pointer to this decision.

The term "Notification" itself gets no `_Client says_` line: nothing in the product shows a Client the literal word today (no UI element, no template built), which puts it beside Visit and Plan Instance in ADR-0005's "no client-facing surface" bucket, not beside Message (which is genuinely shown, `<h2>Messages</h2>`, but happens not to diverge). If a future email template ever surfaces the word itself to a Client — a footer disclaimer, for instance — that text falls under the register like any other Client-facing copy at the point it's written, not in advance of it.
