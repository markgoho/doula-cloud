# The founder goes where doulas already gather, and the site is arrived at

How Doula Cloud reaches its first buyers was charted on the go-to-market map, [#991](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/991), on 2026-09-08: founder voice on every channel through launch; only a platform that will bring paying customers gets an account; no contact with DONA until the teaser is live; one pool of about $2,500 through January; launch-day targets of 150 confirmed subscribers and 5 pilot Practices, recorded as numbers to be wrong about. [#992](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/992) read the DONA Summit's exhibitor terms first-hand, [#993](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/993) surveyed where doulas gather and found the local market is a room a founder can walk into, [#995](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/995) read how the competing tools talk about themselves, and [#996](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/996) found which handles are ours. [ADR-0014](0014-the-waitlist-lives-outside-our-stack.md) put the waitlist in Buttondown for one confirmation and the January sends; [ADR-0016](0016-teaser-analytics-are-cookieless-and-the-channel-rides-on-the-form.md) made a subscriber's channel ride on four hidden `utm_*` fields whose counts are a floor; [ADR-0043](0043-a-date-is-a-trigger-only-when-someone-set-it.md) fixed what the list hears and how every send is measured; the teaser copy fixed on [#362](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/362) says "I'd rather tell you that than show you a demo"; `hugo/layouts/page.html` defaults every page to `noindex, nofollow` with `robots: index` opted into per page; and the pilot terms are unlisted on [#444](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/444). Those records hold the mechanisms. None of them said where the founder goes, what the money buys, or whether the January site has to be found.

On 2026-09-15 the founder answered those three questions against the twenty-nine books on the values track, in the third grilling session under [ADR-0041](0041-a-book-enters-the-repo-as-evidence-and-becomes-a-rule-only-through-a-decision.md), on [#1373](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1373), from the theme synthesis at [`docs/research/books/themes/reaching-the-market.md`](../research/books/themes/reaching-the-market.md). Thirteen questions over two rounds, and one research pass through the founder's own logged-in browser over the Facebook groups #993 could not read. Three times the founder took a position against the recommendation put to him, and the record says so. He confirmed the positions record on that ticket. This ADR is the value behind the plan: why each position holds, in his reasons, with the evidence weighed and what was rejected. Page citations are as the read-backs in `docs/research/books/` give them, each under its own page rule. A book is evidence here and nowhere the reason.

**The founder goes where doulas already gather and speaks there as a person. The money buys presence, never attention. The January site is arrived at, not found: a doula reaches it from a link, a post, or a person who said its name.**

## The decision

### A Bronze booth at the DONA Summit, in the teaser's voice, and no seat

Doula Cloud exhibits at the DONA International Summit on October 26–27, 2026, with a Bronze booth at $400 under the product name, and the founder takes no seat in the sessions. The booth carries the teaser's words and its link, no product screens and no walk of any journey; the founder is in the live chat and on Zoom as a person; the pilot offer is made by hand, pointing at the pilot terms, only to a Practice who asks. Every link carries `utm_source=dona-summit-2026`, `utm_medium=event`, `utm_campaign=teaser`, and `utm_content=` the card live on the day. The application is a contact with DONA, so it waits for the teaser to be live on October 1 and goes by the route the arm's-length rule on [#998](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/998) names; setup closes October 15 and there are no refunds. The work is [#1384](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1384). The founder's reason: being seen under the product name at the national summit, because what a search for the company returns matters. A seat and no booth, and Silver at $750 for a seat and a video slot, were put to him and not taken.

Weighed for: _Zero to One_'s point that selling extends to non-customers, since what a search for the company returns matters ([zero-to-one](../research/books/zero-to-one.md), ch. 11, PDF pp. 104–105); _Make_'s launch where the customers are ([make-the-bootstrappers-handbook](../research/books/make-the-bootstrappers-handbook.md), Launch, pp. 83, 99–101, 107); _Software as a Science_'s founder who reached $200,000 MRR by going deep on one show's mechanics ([software-as-a-science](../research/books/software-as-a-science.md), ch. 3, PDF p. 78); _This Is Marketing_'s Marvel at Comic-Con a year before release, with news rather than a ticket pitch ([this-is-marketing](../research/books/this-is-marketing.md), Ch. 17, PDF p. 156), which is what a booth in the teaser's voice is; Blank and Dorf's rule that this is not the time for public demos ([the-startup-owners-manual](../research/books/the-startup-owners-manual.md), Web/Mobile, Ch. 5, PDF p. 420), held as the rule for what the booth shows.

Weighed against and rejected: _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s claim that business networking is a complete waste of time ([the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant](../research/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant.md), Building Wealth, pp. 85–86); _Lean Marketing_'s day at an industry conference as an attendee ([lean-marketing](../research/books/lean-marketing.md), Chapter 2, pp. 49–50), since the seat was declined. Accepted as the shape rather than rejected: _Lean Marketing_'s trade-show route as one line in a budget (Chapter 2, p. 50; Chapter 8, p. 139). Kept as the read after the fact, not the reason before: _The SaaS Playbook_'s test that an in-person event pays only if its return covers its cost ([the-saas-playbook](../research/books/the-saas-playbook.md), pp. 99–100), read by January on `utm_source=dona-summit-2026` in confirmed subscribers and in any pilot Practice who came through it.

### Two channels through January, one per target

Two channels run through January, one per target. The founder in person in Rochester serves the five-Practice target: the Rochester Doula Cooperative, the Beautiful Birth Choices cohort and its 429-member Western New York Facebook group, and the pilot agency's own doulas, each a room he can walk into. One online doula-business community, Doula Office Hours (3,927 members), serves the 150-subscriber target, on its own rule: the link as a comment in each month's promo thread, October through January, and the founder as a member answering questions in his own name the rest of the month, one visit a week; bebo mia's Strategy & Support group (12,721 members) is second, with one permission email for one announcement. Every other venue on #993 is a place the founder may visit as a person carrying the teaser's link, never a channel with a cadence and an owner. Product Hunt and Hacker News fail #991's test because no doula is there. The group sizes and rules were read on 2026-09-15 through the founder's own browser and are recorded on #1373. The founder's reason: one channel per target; a room of fifty doulas can produce five pilot Practices and cannot produce 150 subscribers. He accepts the consequence that the 150-subscriber target is the number most likely to be wrong. One channel, Rochester alone, was put to him and not taken.

Weighed for: _The Mom Test_'s rule that a good customer segment is a who-where pair ([the-mom-test](../research/books/the-mom-test.md), ch. 7, pp. 94–97); _Obviously Awesome_'s word of mouth in a tight subsegment, the Big Fish, Small Pond case ([obviously-awesome](../research/books/obviously-awesome.md), Step 8, pp. 104–106); _This Is Marketing_'s showing up where a tribe's permission already exists (Ch. 17, PDF p. 156); _The SaaS Playbook_'s one fast and one slow channel (pp. 102–106); _Company of One_'s social capital as a bank account from which nothing is withdrawn that was not deposited ([company-of-one](../research/books/company-of-one.md), ch. 12, PDF pp. 144–145).

Answered rather than rejected, since each target has exactly one channel: _Zero to One_'s power law of distribution and its warning that the kitchen-sink approach fails (ch. 11, PDF p. 104); _Software as a Science_'s one column taken all the way green before the next is stacked (ch. 3, PDF pp. 80–81); Blank and Dorf's one distribution channel with the founder's own website tested in parallel (Web/Mobile, Ch. 3, PDF pp. 275-283; Physical Channel, Ch. 8, PDF p. 992); _The Lean Startup_'s one engine of growth at a time ([the-lean-startup](../research/books/the-lean-startup.md), Ch. 10 Grow, PDF p. 211). Rejected: _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s five sources of new leads ([the-1-page-marketing-plan](../research/books/the-1-page-marketing-plan.md), Chapter 3, pp. 130–132); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s three Top Shelf channels ([start-small-stay-small](../research/books/start-small-stay-small.md), Ch. 5, pp. 145–150); _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s four signal campaigns run together ([entrepreneur-revolution](../research/books/entrepreneur-revolution.md), Chapter 4, PDF pp. 95–96); _Make_'s Product Hunt and Hacker News (Launch, pp. 83, 99–101, 107), which _Software as a Science_ counts as search engines rather than launch venues (ch. 3, PDF p. 71).

### The founder speaks as a person, and no product-name account posts before January

Before January the founder speaks in public as a person, about the work and what doulas tell him, with no demo and no positioning claim until [#999](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/999) has made one. No product-name account posts before January: the handles stay held as #996 found them, a profile may carry the teaser's link, and the first product-name post is launch week's on [#1004](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1004). The founder's one platform is his own Facebook profile. The founder's reason: the positioning ticket is blocked on interviews not yet held, so a product account would be saying what business it is in before he knows; and a product account with nothing to show gets no paying customer, which is #991's own test.

Weighed for: Blank and Dorf's rule that Customer Development and stealth are mutually exclusive and that this is still not the time for press releases, interviews, blogs or public demos, because the founder does not yet know enough to say what business the company is in (Web/Mobile, Ch. 5, PDF p. 420); _The Four Steps to the Epiphany_'s no serious marketing spending before a repeatable sales roadmap ([the-four-steps-to-the-epiphany](../research/books/the-four-steps-to-the-epiphany.md), Chapter 5, pp. 346–347); _Lean Marketing_'s warning that on social media you are a tenant, and its instruction to choose one platform to start (Chapter 13, pp. 212, 216); _This Is Marketing_'s social platforms as a sharecropper's arrangement (Ch. 17, PDF pp. 152-153).

Honored in the founder's own voice rather than rejected: _Make_'s build in public, where showing mistakes makes a founder look human (pp. 118–121; This book, p. 17); _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s media as permissionless leverage (pp. 35, 57, 60); _Company of One_'s out-sharing and teaching as the edge (ch. 9, PDF pp. 106–113). Rejected: full build-in-public with screens.

### The pool buys presence, never attention

The $2,500 pool buys presence only before launch: seats, memberships where a member may post or be listed, and the local coffee. There is no advertising of any kind before launch, and money the plan does not spend is held, not allocated. The first paid test, if there ever is one, is after launch. The founder's reason: no signup has a value until a Practice has bought a Credit, so the bar for a direct ad cannot be met before January, and every threshold the books name sits past a point this company reaches before then.

Weighed for: _Make_'s paid traffic only after traction at 100,000 monthly active users (Grow, pp. 115–117); _The SaaS Playbook_'s funnel arithmetic, high-touch at $500 a month or more and low-touch as a volume game (Marketing, pp. 90–93); _Design for Cognitive Bias_'s eBay and Bing figures, paid search read as a loss even at scale ([design-for-cognitive-bias](../research/books/design-for-cognitive-bias.md), ch. 3, pp. 45–47); _Software as a Science_'s rarely recommending paid ads as a first channel for a self-funded company (ch. 3, PDF p. 74); _This Is Marketing_'s no direct ads until a value can be assigned to each step (Ch. 15, PDF pp. 140-141); _The Four Steps to the Epiphany_ (Chapter 5, pp. 346–347); _Forget the Funnel_'s two cases that grew without paid acquisition ([forget-the-funnel](../research/books/forget-the-funnel.md), ch. 1, PDF pp. 17, 21); _The Lean Startup_'s paid engine, whose margin competition bids away (Ch. 10 Grow, PDF pp. 209-211); _The Voltage Effect_'s read of the last dollar rather than the average ([the-voltage-effect](../research/books/the-voltage-effect.md), Chapter 7, PDF pp. 155–156, 161–162), kept for the first test. Deferred to after launch: _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s testing-phase budget, fail often and fail cheap (Chapter 3, pp. 128–129).

Weighed against and rejected: _Zero to One_'s defense of advertising as something that works and leaves impressions (ch. 11, PDF pp. 97, 102–103), on the same book's distribution doldrums near a low-priced product sold to small businesses (ch. 11, PDF p. 101); _Lean Marketing_'s long-standing advertisers in a trade publication who are likely getting a return (Chapter 2, pp. 49–50), on _Start Small, Stay Small_'s two reasons against magazine advertising, prospects hard to track and copy fixed mid-run (Ch. 2, pp. 47, 57), and because ADR-0016 can read a `utm_source` on a link and cannot read a printed page; a doula group's sold sponsorship, on the same rule.

### The January site is arrived at, and findable by name

The January site is arrived at: every visitor comes from a link, a post or a person. It is findable by name: the home page and the pricing page opt into `robots: index`, so a doula who heard the name in a room in Rochester can type it and find it, while every other page keeps the layout's `noindex, nofollow` default. The words are a doula's own, which [#868](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/868) already requires. Nothing content-shaped is written before launch. The founder's reason: the channel is a room, not a search box, and a name search is the one search the site has to win.

Weighed for: _This Is Marketing_'s two steps, make a product worth searching for specifically and then be the one they want to find, against the delusion of being the first match among a thousand pages (Ch. 15, PDF pp. 143-144); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s finding that a ten-page static site and a ten-page blog that never changes look the same to Google, with directories, article repositories and bought links recorded as diminished, duplicated or penalized (Ch. 5, pp. 153–154, 159–163); _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s and _Zero to One_'s measure of discoverability by what a search for the name returns (Chapter 8, PDF pp. 173–175; ch. 11, PDF p. 104); _Jobs To Be Done_'s buyers who enter a job step or a desired outcome rather than a product name ([jobs-to-be-done](../research/books/jobs-to-be-done.md), ch. 4 §IX, pp. 115–116) and _Lean Marketing_'s searcher who did not type the generic word (Chapter 2, p. 38), which is why the words are hers.

Deferred to after launch, out of scope on #991: _Software as a Science_'s awareness and consideration keywords, and its warning that a company writing only about its own software addresses a tenth of its opportunity (ch. 3, PDF pp. 71–73; ch. 4, PDF pp. 88–89); _Company of One_'s teaching content that does not sell (ch. 9, PDF pp. 106–113). Rejected: `noindex` through launch, and awareness pages written before January.

### The site's one ask is "start", and proof is founding facts only

The January site's one ask is "start": a Practice created with three free Credits. `hello@doula.cloud`, which the pilot terms already publish as reaching a person, is the transitional action. There is no lead magnet and no second list, and the teaser's waitlist form comes down when the site opens. Proof is founding facts only, when it opened and where it was built, with no testimonial, no count and no trusted-by row; whether a one-line "who this is not for" sentence is written is #868's copy call. The founder's reason for the ask: the free account is the capture, it costs her a real family to use, and the fourteen-day message on [#1381](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1381) is the one nudge the record allows, so the doula who is not ready can sit on three Credits and is not lost; an address captured on a second form would go on a list the product may not nurture under ADR-0043, and #991 puts post-launch email out of scope, so the capture has no reader. His reason for the proof: the pilot is private (#444), and a pilot Practice's name on the site makes it public; her words are earned after launch, not asked for before it. One named pilot Practice's words with her consent, asked in December, was put to him and not taken.

Weighed for: _Forget the Funnel_'s Try-it-free call to action replacing Schedule-a-demo (ch. 8, PDF pp. 113–114); _Landing Page Hot Tips_'s free teaser and no obstacles to a demo ([landing-page-hot-tips](../research/books/landing-page-hot-tips.md), Tips #40, #46; PDF pp. 83, 91); _The Mom Test_'s Buffer, where the conversations rather than the conversion rate convinced the founder (ch. 6, p. 78); [ADR-0042](0042-a-practice-pays-per-engagement-and-doula-cloud-is-paid-when-the-doula-is.md)'s finding that spending a Credit costs her a real family run through the product; _Lean Marketing_'s collect only what is essential, because each field reduces completion (Chapter 8, p. 141; Chapter 9, pp. 148–149); _Building a StoryBrand 2.0_'s transitional call to action beside the direct one ([building-a-storybrand-2](../research/books/building-a-storybrand-2.md), ch. 14, PDF pp. 176–178; ch. 15, PDF pp. 192–193), honored by the reply address; _Competing Against Luck_'s obligation to convey who should not hire a product ([competing-against-luck](../research/books/competing-against-luck.md), ch. 6, PDF p. 131), handed to #868; #995's finding that no competing tool publishes how many doulas use it.

Weighed against and rejected: _Start Small, Stay Small_'s email address as the website's first goal, won with a reward (Ch. 4, pp. 118–119, 135–138); _Building a StoryBrand 2.0_'s lead generator and drip (ch. 15, PDF pp. 198–205); _Marketing Made Simple_'s lead-generating PDF with a hit rate of roughly six in ten ([marketing-made-simple](../research/books/marketing-made-simple.md), ch. 6, pp. 108–110, 125); _Software as a Science_'s lead magnet (ch. 3, PDF pp. 69–70); _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s lead capture between the free gift and the first paid step (Chapter 5, PDF pp. 122–123); _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s never sell from the ad, because only about 3 percent are ready to buy now (Chapter 4, pp. 140–143), answered by the free account being the capture; _Landing Page Hot Tips_'s social proof, usage figures and best testimonial above the fold (Tips #52, #82, #83; PDF pp. 100, 153, 154–155); _Copyhackers: Uplift_'s trusted-by row ([copyhackers-uplift](../research/books/copyhackers-uplift.md), Section 2, pp. 21–22).

### No cold email

The Summit's attendee list is used only to write once, personally, to the people the founder actually spoke with in the chat or on Zoom. Nobody on the Monroe County Medicaid directory gets a cold email; the pilot agency and the Rochester Doula Cooperative are the way into the room. The founder's reason: a note to someone he spoke with is an anticipated, personal message; a note to the rest of a list that came through DONA is a gamble with the arm's-length rule on it; and what cold outreach is said to teach, the room twenty minutes away teaches better.

Weighed for: _This Is Marketing_'s permission as the privilege of anticipated, personal and relevant messages (Ch. 17, PDF pp. 152-154); _The Mom Test_'s cold approaches as a different beast and a gamble, and its caveat that calling in old favors is annoying and can burn bridges (ch. 6, pp. 82, 85); _The Four Steps to the Epiphany_'s launch through messengers narrowed to the small group carrying the most influence (Chapter 5, pp. 404–412); _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s founder who sold face to face and says the meetings produced the language and the objections a website would not (Chapter 4, PDF p. 101). Rejected: _Software as a Science_'s cold outreach for the cash-poor, time-rich founder without a network (ch. 3, PDF pp. 75–76).

### The targets are numbers to be wrong about

The launch-day targets, 150 confirmed subscribers and 5 pilot Practices, are numbers to be wrong about, not promises, as #991 recorded them. No book's uncited figure is the reason for any position above. #991 recorded the targets that way, which is _Thinking in Bets_' rule that a forecast is not shown wrong by the likelier future failing to arrive and that a guessed number is stated as a guess ([thinking-in-bets](../research/books/thinking-in-bets.md), ch. 1, PDF p. 33; ch. 6, PDF p. 166). Walling's ten-times figure and Levels's thresholds are the authors' own experience; _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_ disowns its follow-up statistics in two footnotes (Chapter 5, p. 171; Chapter 8, p. 263); _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s 1-in-20 is what its author sees across his own client base (Chapter 4, PDF pp. 96, 104). Each is weighed above as experience, none as a measurement. What is read is the act on a `utm_source` (ADR-0016; ADR-0043), and the group sizes on #1373 are each group's own number as shown to a non-member.

## What was considered instead

**A seat at the Summit and no booth.** Put to the founder and not taken: the booth is the way to be seen under the product name, which a seat does not give.

**Silver, for a seat and a video slot.** Put and not taken: the founder does not sit in the sessions, so Bronze is the whole spend.

**One channel, Rochester alone.** Put and not taken: a room of fifty doulas cannot produce 150 subscribers, and each target now has one channel.

**Five lead sources, or three, or four campaigns at once.** Rejected because several channels attempted and none nailed is the failure _Zero to One_ names, and two targets need two channels, not five.

**A product-name account before January, and build-in-public with screens.** Rejected because positioning is blocked on interviews, so the account would be saying what business it is in before the founder knows, and a product account with nothing to show brings no paying customer.

**Any advertising before launch, including a trade-publication ad and a doula group's sponsorship.** Rejected because no signup has a value yet, a printed page cannot carry a `utm_source`, and the remainder of the pool is worth more held.

**Awareness pages before January, or `noindex` through launch.** Rejected because the channel is a room and the one search the site must win is its own name.

**A lead magnet, a second list, or a surviving waitlist form.** Rejected because the free account is the capture and a captured address would have no reader.

**One named pilot Practice's words on the site, or a pilot count.** Put and not taken: the pilot is private, her words are earned after launch, and five reads as small.

**Cold email to the attendee list or the Monroe County directory.** Rejected because permission is anticipated and personal, the list came through DONA under the arm's-length rule, and the room teaches what cold outreach is said to teach.
