# A Practice carries one timezone, and it decides what a calendar day means

A Visit's type is decided by comparing two things that are not the same shape. On one side is the Visit's own instant — `scheduled_at`, or `created_at` for a Visit nobody has scheduled — a `timestamptz`, a point on the world's clock. On the other is the Engagement's `pregnancy_ended_on`, a `date`, which carries no zone at all and never did: somebody typed the day the pregnancy ended, and a day is not an instant. Until this decision, `visit.DeriveType` closed that gap by formatting the instant in UTC. Nothing chose UTC; it was what was left when the schema held no zone to choose. A Doula in Rochester who worked a birth at 9pm on the fifteenth had that Visit typed against the sixteenth, so the product told her the evening she spent at the birth was postpartum care. Filed as [#953](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/953), out of [#281](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/281)'s own doc comment naming the limitation while it built the derivation.

**A Practice carries one timezone, and every calendar-day comparison in its data happens in it.** `practices.timezone`, an IANA zone name. It is a fact about the business, at the same level as its rate card and its payment terms, and one Practice has one of it however many zones its Staff sit in. The comparison it fixes is not really about zones at all: it is that a `date` column means a day, and a day only exists somewhere.

**Not the reader's own zone.** This is the alternative that looks most helpful and is the most wrong. CONTEXT.md's **Visit** entry has one function compute the type on every read, and the same entry is built on no two surfaces disagreeing. A reader-local zone breaks that outright: an Admin in Denver and the Doula in Rochester would open the same Engagement and read two different types for one Visit, with nothing in the model able to say which was right. Worse, it would move — the same person traveling would watch a Visit retype itself. A Practice's record of its own care is not allowed to depend on where the person reading it is standing. `app/src/lib/dates.ts`'s `formatScheduledVisit` still renders a Visit's clock time in the reader's browser zone, and whether *display* should follow the Practice's clock or the reader's is a genuinely separate question with a genuinely different answer available; #953's own Out of scope section holds it, and this decision does not settle it.

**Not a zone per Visit.** A zone on the Visit row is the technically richest answer and it belongs to the richer time model — an end time, a status, a location — that CONTEXT.md's **Visit** entry parks on [Calendar: scheduling, booking, and availability for Visits (#330)](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/330). Building half of it here, for the one field that would answer this question, would put a fact on the row that no screen writes and no person states, and #330 would then have to unpick it.

**The column is `text`, and its default is a stopgap.** Not an enum and not a `CHECK` against `pg_timezone_names`: the IANA database is revised several times a year, so an enum would need a migration per revision, and `pg_timezone_names` is not immutable, so a `CHECK` cannot call it. Validation belongs on the write side, where `time.LoadLocation` is the same check the read already performs. `00110_practice_timezone.sql` lands the column `NOT NULL DEFAULT 'America/New_York'` and — unlike every other migration under `guardrail_test.go`'s DEFAULT-then-DROP rule — keeps the default, because nothing on the signup path asks for a zone yet and every `INSERT` into `practices` would fail the moment it went away. `America/New_York` is grounded in the product being US-only today (Work State enumerates US states and nothing else) and in the pilot being a Rochester, New York agency. It was a placeholder for the window before [#1166](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1166) let an Owner state a zone, not a claim that the product gets to pick a Practice's clock. Pre-launch, that window had no Practice in it, and #1166 closed it: self-signup asks the founder which zone her Practice keeps its calendar in and writes it on the `INSERT`, an Owner or Admin changes it afterward from the Practice's own settings, and `00113_practice_timezone_stated.sql` drops the `DEFAULT` so an `INSERT` that names no zone fails loudly against the `NOT NULL` rather than quietly inheriting New York. The validation that decision reserved for the write side is `api/internal/ianazone`, one leaf package both write paths call: it refuses anything the IANA database does not name, and refuses the two names `time.LoadLocation` answers without consulting the database at all — `""`, which is UTC and would turn an omitted field into a deliberate-looking choice, and `"Local"`, which is whichever zone the process happens to run in and would make one stored string mean different days on different hosts. **Who may state it, and what changing it does.** Owner and Admin, declared at the mount, the same authority that writes the rate card and the payment terms — the level this decision already puts a Practice's zone at. Changing it is retroactive by construction and that is the intended behavior, not a hazard to guard against: the type is computed on every read, so no instant moves and nothing stored is migrated, but a Visit worked late in the evening can move between `birth` and `postpartum` the moment the zone changes. The settings screen states that consequence before the press rather than reporting it after. What is **not** settled is a Practice that genuinely relocates and wants its old Visits typed in the old zone and its new ones in the new: that needs a per-Visit or effective-dated zone, which is #330's richer time model, and #1166 was scoped to stating the one zone with this constraint written down.

**A zone that will not load is an error, never a fall back to UTC.** The Visit list refuses with a 500 rather than answering, and `DeriveType` takes its zone as required rather than standing UTC in for a missing one. A silent fall back would put the derivation back exactly where it was — the wrong day, and nothing said about it — which is the failure this decision exists to end. The package that loads a zone also takes a blank `time/tzdata` import, so the IANA database travels in the binary as `time.LoadLocation`'s fallback. The API image, `gcr.io/distroless/static-debian12`, does ship `/usr/share/zoneinfo` today — checked by exporting the image and listing it, rather than assumed either way — so this is not repairing a broken image. It is declining to depend on a base image's contents for a fact the product would otherwise get wrong in silence.

**The scope of this decision is `visit.DeriveType`, and the rest is named rather than assumed.** The other calendar-day comparison in the BFF is the manual Payment's "paidOn cannot be in the future" guard, which compares against UTC today and so accepts a genuinely-tomorrow date for the last hours of every Eastern day; it is [#1167](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1167), not a silent change here. Everything else the codebase formats as `YYYY-MM-DD` is a filename or an RFC3339 rendering, not a comparison, and is untouched.
