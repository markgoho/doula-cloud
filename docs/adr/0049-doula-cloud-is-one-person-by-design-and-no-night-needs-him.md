# Doula Cloud is one person by design, and no night needs him

Who works on Doula Cloud, who owns it and whose money is in it were decided one fact at a time before anyone named the value. [#375](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/375) settled a single-member New York LLC, a disregarded entity with the S-corp election ruled out and not deferred, an LLC and not a C-corp with a C-corp only if the funding position changed, no cofounder taking equity, no employee on payroll and no outside investment "before January", and the founder working the professional questions himself with [#383](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/383) as the one ticket naming an hour of counsel worth buying; it parked "what changes when the first person is hired" as fog and sharpened it once, that an employee working from another state carries physical presence with her and so a second sales-tax registration on day one ([`docs/research/ny-llc-tax-obligations.md`](../research/ny-llc-tax-obligations.md)). [#29](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/29) recorded a solo developer building with AI assistance. [ADR-0014](0014-the-waitlist-lives-outside-our-stack.md) costed work in founder sessions rather than dollars. [ADR-0044](0044-the-founder-goes-where-doulas-already-gather-and-the-site-is-arrived-at.md) P4 set a pool of about $2,500 through January that buys presence and never attention. [ADR-0046](0046-the-product-counts-what-a-practice-did-and-never-who-she-is.md) P3 and P4 made the month the business is in the black the only business number, read once a month by hand, with no figure on the page a goal. [#449](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/449) has founding grants issued by hand with the issuer recorded, and migration 00055 named that issuer "the platform operator" because the person is none of [ADR-0022](0022-one-activity-log-with-a-subject-and-three-kinds-of-actor.md)'s three actor kinds. [ADR-0048](0048-nothing-holds-a-practice-here-but-the-next-family.md) P2 committed the founder himself to answering `hello@doula.cloud` with no ticket system and no bot in front of him, through the pilot and the first year, and left the hours and what happens when he cannot answer to this record. `/support` and the pilot terms publish "That address reaches a person" and nothing about time ([`docs/copy/support-page.md`](../copy/support-page.md)). The Google Cloud BAA exists in the founder's own name ([#336](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/336); [#546](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/546) re-executes it in the LLC's). [ADR-0045](0045-a-doula-is-heard-before-she-is-chosen.md) P5 made the founder's own knowledge evidence of how the work is done and never of the market. What insurance to carry and what event binds it was open on [#386](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/386). Those records hold the mechanisms. None of them said how many hours a week the founder gives the business or whether his employment continues, whether Doula Cloud is one person by design or one person for now, whether the three "before January" facts are standing rules, how much of his own money goes in before the business has to pay its own way, who is woken when the product is down at three in the morning during a birth, whether the daughters' work is a plan, whether the refusal of a CPA and an attorney is the rule for every professional and every contractor, or what a first hire changes.

On 2026-09-19 the founder answered those questions against the twenty-nine books on the values track, in the eighth and last grilling session under [ADR-0041](0041-a-book-enters-the-repo-as-evidence-and-becomes-a-rule-only-through-a-decision.md), on [#1378](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1378), from the theme synthesis at [`docs/research/books/themes/founder-time-hiring-funding.md`](../research/books/themes/founder-time-hiring-funding.md). Sixteen questions over two rounds. Nine recommendations were taken as written; two were corrected by the founder in his own words, on the bot at `hello@` and on the night alert, and the corrected positions were put back to him and taken; one answer named a fact only he holds, the hours; one named a number, the cap; one was withheld from the record at his request and is carried here only as what the positions record keeps of it. He confirmed the positions record on that ticket. This ADR is the value behind those records: why each position holds, in his reasons, with the evidence weighed and what was rejected. Page citations are as the read-backs in `docs/research/books/` give them, each under its own page rule. A book is evidence here and nowhere the reason.

**Doula Cloud is one person by design, and no night needs him. The founder builds it in the hours beside his employment with the agents he runs, owns all of it, takes nobody's money, and answers `hello@` himself within a day with nothing in front of him; the product is built so that no failure at three in the morning needs a person; and help, when the wall is hit, is a person paid for a named piece of work and never an employee.**

## The decision

### Ten to fifteen hours a week, beside employment that continues

Doula Cloud is built and run in ten to fifteen hours a week of the founder's own time, spent directing the agents that do the building, beside employment that continues through the pilot and the first year, and no trigger for leaving that employment is set. Whether he ever goes full-time is not a question before the January 2028 page shows twelve months of Credits bought, since the month the business is in the black is the only business number (ADR-0046 P3, P4) and every target stays a prediction (ADR-0044 P8). The employment is what lets the business refuse outside money (the fifth position below): it carries the household's income and its health insurance. The founder's reason, in his words: "I spend about 10-15 hours a week on doulacloud interacting with you, Claude, who in turn spends many hours building"; the employment continuing was recommended, not contradicted, and confirmed with the positions record.

Weighed for: _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s lifestyle business with a healthy income as enough to count ([entrepreneur-revolution](../research/books/entrepreneur-revolution.md), Chapter 4, PDF pp. 91–92); _Company of One_'s concession that a large employer's benefits are real and that insurance costs more outside a group plan ([company-of-one](../research/books/company-of-one.md), ch. 13, PDF p. 163; ch. 11, PDF pp. 131–132), and its company of one that profits outside the hours worked rather than trading time for money one-to-one (Prologue, PDF p. 10); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s building your own product as a great option for a founder with no money and plenty of time ([start-small-stay-small](../research/books/start-small-stay-small.md), Ch. 3, p. 94); _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s code as permissionless leverage that earns while you sleep ([the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant](../research/books/the-almanack-of-naval-ravikant.md), Building Wealth, pp. 34–35, 58, 60); _Buy Back Your Time_'s wearing every hat as inevitable early ([buy-back-your-time](../research/books/buy-back-your-time.md), ch. 1, PDF pp. 32–33). Rejected: _Zero to One_'s everyone involved should be full-time ([zero-to-one](../research/books/zero-to-one.md), ch. 9, PDF p. 86), because it is written for a venture-backed company paying salaries and this one pays none; its warning that not everyone talented should found a company (ch. 7, PDF pp. 69–71), because the founding is done; _Start Small, Stay Small_'s dollarized hour as a reason to buy back time (Ch. 1, pp. 26–28), because the founder's hours here are not for sale.

### One person by design, and the promise survives the person

Doula Cloud is one person by design: the founder, the agents he runs, and a person paid for a named piece of work. Nobody is hired to grow the business, a person is ever paid only so a promise already made keeps being kept, and what survives the founder is the promise, not the person. ADR-0048 P2's "through the pilot and the first year" is how far that record promised and not a plan to change; "a person answers" is the product's, and if the person is ever not the founder, she inherits the refusals, no ticket system and no bot in front of her. This settles the synthesis's "whether one person is a ceiling or a design" conflict on the design side, and its "whether the founder or the system carries it" conflict by splitting the day from the night: the founder carries the day (the third position) and the system carries the night (the fourth). The founder's reason, in his words: "yes, exactly"; the position rests on the reasoning put to him, that the founder is not the moat (ADR-0048 P1), the feature list follows from the Engagement (ADR-0048 P4), the pool buys presence and never attention (ADR-0044 P4), and no figure on the monthly page is a goal (ADR-0046 P4), so there is nothing a second person would be hired to grow.

Weighed for: _Company of One_'s staying small as an end goal rather than a stepping stone, its four traits that raise revenue, enjoyment, focus and experience while refusing to raise payroll, expenses and stress, and its Startup Genome finding that 74 percent of high-growth tech startup failures came from scaling too quickly (Prologue, PDF p. 10; ch. 1, PDF pp. 15, 18–27; ch. 2, PDF pp. 30–31, 35); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s Micropreneur, who stays solo with no employees under any circumstance (Ch. 1, pp. 1–2); _Make_'s avoid hiring and build robots, because a hire makes the founder liable for their income ([make-the-bootstrappers-handbook](../research/books/make-the-bootstrappers-handbook.md), Build, pp. 41–43; Automate, pp. 172, 177); _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s labor as the worst form of leverage (Building Wealth, pp. 34–35); _The Voltage Effect_'s legitimate reasons for staying small or medium-sized ([the-voltage-effect](../research/books/the-voltage-effect.md), Conclusion, PDF pp. 216–217); _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s presumption of success leading to premature scaling as a deadly sin ([the-startup-owners-manual](../research/books/the-startup-owners-manual.md), Strategy Guide, Ch. 1, PDF pp. 41–53). Rejected: _The Voltage Effect_'s limit on how big a business resting on one uncloneable person can get (Chapter 3, PDF pp. 69, 74) as a problem to design past, because the ceiling is the design and the moat is not the person; _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s scout team of two and its founders who get too much credit (Chapter 6, PDF pp. 129–133; Chapter 1, PDF p. 18), because there is no second person and the record refuses a cofounder; _The SaaS Playbook_'s challenge to a founder who does not plan to hire, to hire at least a support person ([the-saas-playbook](../research/books/the-saas-playbook.md), Team, pp. 117–126), because the work it names is the founder's own promise; _Buy Back Your Time_'s Replacement Ladder (ch. 5, PDF pp. 99–108), because every rung is a person hired to grow; _The Lean Startup_'s entrepreneur as an actual job title inside large organizations ([the-lean-startup](../research/books/the-lean-startup.md), Ch. 12 Innovate, PDF pp. 254–256), because there is no organization for the title to sit in.

### A person answers within a day, and a program works behind him and never in front of her

The founder reads `hello@doula.cloud` once a day, every day, at a time he chooses, and answers within one day; the rule is recorded here and published nowhere. A program may work behind him and never in front of her, and those are the record's two words. A bot in front of her speaks to her, acknowledging, answering, asking her to wait, closing or deciding, and is refused, as ADR-0048 P2 already had it. A bot behind him speaks only to him and is allowed: it may sort, label, route, summarize and draft, and may never send, answer, acknowledge, close, delay or drop a message; every message is read by the founder, every reply leaves under his name by his act, and a summary sits beside a message and never instead of it. When he is asleep she hears from him in the morning. When he is away more than a day he sets an away notice by his own act, naming the day he is back, and takes it down himself, which is [ADR-0043](0043-a-date-is-a-trigger-only-when-someone-set-it.md) P1's date fixed by a person's act and not a bot, since it speaks only because he said he was away. Nothing is published beyond "reaches a person", which `/support` and the pilot terms already carry and which passes [ADR-0047](0047-a-doula-sees-what-moves-her-and-her-own-words-do-the-moving.md) P1's test: a number is a promise measured by a clock that one person cannot keep on the night of a family birth, and nobody would measure it, since ADR-0046 P4 refuses a complaint count. The GOV.UK contact pattern, the reference under [ADR-0021](0021-govuk-is-the-reference-for-service-patterns.md), states a response time; the departure is recorded here with that reason, that a department with a rota can keep the promise and a company of one cannot make it honestly. The founder's reason, in his words: "I can't promise I won't use a bot for classification of incoming email, but I will always be the person _behind_ the bot", and "yes" to the rest.

Weighed for: _Company of One_'s structural advantage that its service does not scale and does not have to (ch. 7, PDF pp. 86–87), weighed as evidence that a founder reading it himself is what a company of one can do, the reason being his, above; _This Is Marketing_'s person taking responsibility for the work against a faceless corporation speaking in the second person ([this-is-marketing](../research/books/this-is-marketing.md), Ch. 6, PDF p. 67); _The Mom Test_'s hired gun whose bad news is hard to assimilate and whose lukewarm signal is tempting to gloss over ([the-mom-test](../research/books/the-mom-test.md), ch. 8, p. 104), weighed on the rule that a summary never replaces a message, which rests on ADR-0045's evidence rule, since every message at `hello@` is the pilot's evidence; _Make_'s robots (Automate, pp. 172, 177); _Company of One_'s process rather than hiring where more work arrives (ch. 3, PDF pp. 45–46). Rejected: _Buy Back Your Time_'s inbox in the Delegation quadrant and its administrative assistant on the calendar and the inbox as the nonnegotiable first hire (ch. 2, PDF pp. 41, 44–51, 57; ch. 6, PDF pp. 118, 121–124, 129), because the inbox is the promise; _Start Small, Stay Small_'s support as among the easiest things to outsource (Ch. 3, pp. 94, 96), for the same reason; _Company of One_'s CDBaby within two rings and Basecamp within fifteen minutes (ch. 7, PDF pp. 86–87) as a published number; the GOV.UK stated time; a standing auto-acknowledgment, which is the bot in front of her; the session's own recommendation of no bot at all, corrected by the founder.

### No night needs a person

Doula Cloud is built so that no night needs a person: managed services that restart themselves, a database with nightly backups and point-in-time recovery, and an uptime check each on the app and the API that alert the founder's phone in his waking hours only, so every night's failure is found at the start of the day and nothing wakes him. Read from the live project on 2026-09-19: the API runs on Cloud Run with at least one instance always on and up to twenty; the database is Cloud SQL on the shared `db-f1-micro` tier in one zone, with nightly backups, seven kept, seven days of transaction-log retention set and point-in-time recovery not yet switched on, which #1403 turns on; there was no uptime check, no alert policy and no notification channel. The single-zone database, which has no standby, and for which a standby means a dedicated-core tier at several times the database's cost, is an accepted risk through the pilot and the first year, and the standby's cost is the threshold set in advance: it is bought the month the page on ADR-0046 P3 shows Credits bought covering it, and not before. A Practice is told nothing by any of this, there is no status page, no uptime figure goes on the monthly page, and what she hears at three in the morning when the product is down is nothing; the promise stays a person who answers within a day. The uptime check is the same kind of thing as the third position's program, a robot that never speaks to a Practice. The build is [#1403](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1403). The founder's reason, in his words: "I want to build a system that doesn't require my input at 3am", and "yes" to the design.

Weighed for: _Make_'s robots and its maker who fixes bugs over coffee, in the morning (Build, pp. 41–43; Automate, pp. 172, 177); _The Voltage Effect_'s non-negotiable, that where a digital interface must be high quality mediocre engineers are not hired, bounded by its unsustainable costs as one of the five vital signs (Chapter 5, PDF pp. 111, 122); _Company of One_'s process rather than hiring (ch. 3, PDF pp. 45–46); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s automation threshold set in advance at the day a bill exceeds a stated amount (Ch. 6, pp. 166–170), the shape the standby's threshold takes. Rejected: the session's own recommendation of a check that wakes him at any hour, corrected by the founder; no check, because a failure nobody knows about until a doula writes is a day lost; a standby now, because it is several times the database's cost against a $4,000 cap (the sixth position) and a pilot of five Practices; a status page, because it counts nothing and nobody reads it at three in the morning; _Start Small, Stay Small_'s team or firm for recurring critical work (Ch. 6, p. 179), because the critical work here is answered by a check and one person.

### One owner, standing

Doula Cloud has one owner, the founder, and that does not change: no cofounder ever, no equity, option or profit share to anyone, no investor's money in any form, and the C-corp door #375 left ajar closes. Anyone ever paid is paid in cash for work done. Nobody in the founder's household is named anywhere in the record, and no partner of any kind holds a role, a title, equity or access. The three facts #375 settled "before January" to choose an entity are standing rules with no date on them. The founder's reason, in his words: "correct", and, of the household, an answer withdrawn at his request; the position rests on the reasoning put to him, that the model paid when the doula is ([ADR-0042](0042-a-practice-pays-per-engagement-and-doula-cloud-is-paid-when-the-doula-is.md) P1) has no growth to sell an investor, that the pool buys presence and never attention (ADR-0044 P4), that no figure is a goal (ADR-0046 P4), that the founder is not the moat (ADR-0048 P1), and that each refusal in ADR-0048 is one an investor's portfolio would ask to relax.

Weighed for: _The SaaS Playbook_'s cofounder who is not needed and is the largest equity ever given away (Team, pp. 135–141); _Make_'s working alone early because collaborations can be very dangerous, and its refusal to pay a developer in equity for an idea (Idea, pp. 31–32; Build, p. 48); _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s owned equity and its capital as permissioned leverage someone else has to grant (Building Wealth, pp. 31, 34–35, 53–54); _Company of One_'s investors as one of the four reasons companies grow at all, its almost 86 percent of long-term successes having taken no venture money, and Danielle LaPorte pushed out of her own business six months after taking $400,000 (ch. 4, PDF pp. 55–56; ch. 2, PDF p. 31; ch. 1, PDF p. 18); _Start Small, Stay Small_'s investor pitch skipped by design (Preface, PDF p. 9); _Obviously Awesome_'s outside funding on the cost side of two of its three positioning styles, which a bootstrapper may not want ([obviously-awesome](../research/books/obviously-awesome.md), Step 8, pp. 99–101, 115); _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s investor who holds a portfolio of startups against a founder who holds a portfolio of one, and its Manifesto Rule 12, preserve all cash while searching (Web/Mobile, Ch. 4, PDF p. 394; Strategy Guide, Ch. 3, PDF pp. 87–88); _Zero to One_'s fewer than 1 percent of new US businesses each year receiving venture funding (ch. 7, PDF p. 69); _Thinking in Bets_' group recruited to think with rather than to work, on a charter of accuracy over confirmation ([thinking-in-bets](../research/books/thinking-in-bets.md), ch. 4, PDF pp. 103, 105–106), the only form a partner takes here. Rejected: _Zero to One_'s choosing whom to start with as a first decision likened to marriage, and its equity as the compensation that orients people to the future (ch. 9, PDF pp. 82–83, 87–88), because equity orients a person to a growth this company refuses and the founder keeps the whole of it; _Entrepreneur Revolution_'s entrepreneurship as about the team rather than the founder and its no self-made millionaire (Chapter 1, PDF p. 18; Chapter 3, PDF p. 76), because the risk here is one household's hours and not a shared bet; _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s IMVU founders recruiting a professional CEO at the handoff (Strategy Guide, Ch. 2, PDF p. 70), because a company of one has no handoff; _Forget the Funnel_'s two companies grown sharply on a seed round ([forget-the-funnel](../research/books/forget-the-funnel.md), ch. 1, PDF pp. 17, 21), because both sold growth; _Company of One_'s concession that capital can bring mentorship, connections and experience (ch. 4, PDF pp. 61–62), because advice is bought by the hour (the eighth position) and never with equity; _Software as a Science_'s frameworks that hold either way ([software-as-a-science](../research/books/software-as-a-science.md), Introduction, PDF pp. 13, 20), true and no reason; _The SaaS Playbook_'s profit sharing for a run-it-forever company (Team, pp. 135–141), taken one step further to no share at all; a rule reread on a date, because that is ADR-0043's elapsed clock applied to the business itself; a named adviser role for anyone, because a title is a claim on the business this position refuses.

### His own money, capped

The money is the founder's own, from his employment, and the cap for 2027 is $4,000, everything included: the $2,500 pool (ADR-0044 P4), Google Cloud, Buttondown, Pirsch, the domain, the LLC's filing, publication, agent and fees, and the insurance on the ninth position. The cap is a budget on the founder and never a target for the business: a floor on revenue is a goal and refused under ADR-0046 P4, and a ceiling on his own money is the other side of the same page. Reaching it forces nothing; it is his signal to reread the monthly page with the cap beside it. Going past it, or spending on any paid channel after launch, is a ticket that names the count it expects, Credits bought by Practices who arrived from that channel, and the count read afterward on the monthly page (ADR-0046 P2), so "we see value" is a fact in the record and not a feeling; the count is a prediction to be wrong about (ADR-0044 P8) and never a goal. Every cost is paid from the LLC's account once it exists, so the page reads the bank (ADR-0046 P3). The founder's reason, in his words: "yes, $2500, but could easily go more if we see value in it (FB ads, etc.)", corrected in round two to "cap is $4k" once the pool and the cap were set apart.

Weighed for: _Company of One_'s observation that most businesses set a floor on a goal and none on the ceiling, and its proposal to state both (ch. 2, PDF pp. 39–40); _The Voltage Effect_'s unsustainable costs and its check of what it will cost to provide (Chapter 5, PDF p. 111); _The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_'s risk of ruin avoided (Building Wealth, pp. 34, 52, 66–67); _Start Marketing the Day You Start Coding_'s expenses a founder forgets, entity filing fees, an accountant, a lawyer, errors-and-omissions insurance, health and disability insurance, the employer half of FICA ([start-marketing-the-day-you-start-coding](../research/books/start-marketing-the-day-you-start-coding.md), "Expenses", pp. 81–83), as the list the cap covers; _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s Rule 12 and its definition of scalable, one more marketing dollar returning more gross profit than it cost (Strategy Guide, Ch. 3, PDF pp. 87–88), which is the count the ticket names; _Software as a Science_'s paid ads rarely recommended as a first channel for a self-funded company (ch. 3, PDF p. 74). Rejected: no cap, because a business with no ceiling on its owner's money cannot say what it costs to provide; a cap with a consequence, because a consequence on a date is the clock ADR-0043 refuses; judgment with nothing written, because a spend with no count beside it is the one line on the monthly page nobody could read; _Marketing Made Simple_'s founder who lost his savings in a failed investment ([marketing-made-simple](../research/books/marketing-made-simple.md), ch. 1, pp. 12–14), held only as what the cap prevents; _The Startup Owner's Manual_'s per-stage burn rates (Strategy Guide, Ch. 6, PDF p. 141), because there is no stage and no burn; _The 1-Page Marketing Plan_'s whole plan written for a business without an advertising budget ([the-1-page-marketing-plan](../research/books/the-1-page-marketing-plan.md), Introduction, pp. 30–33) as a rule, because ADR-0044 P4 allows a paid test after launch.

### The daughters are an option under one rule

The founder's daughters are an option and not a plan, under one rule: a daughter may be paid for real work that touches no Practice's data and no Client, the site, drawings, copy read aloud, the sandbox tested as a stranger, at a stated hourly rate with hours written down, and never the support inbox, the product's records or anything under the BAA. #375 kept the entity disregarded partly so that a child under 18 can work for a parent's sole proprietorship with no Social Security or Medicare tax on the wages, which is the only hire the record has ever priced. In the record she is the founder's daughter paid for named work, never Staff, Owner, contractor or employee, which are the Practice's words in `CONTEXT.md` and [ADR-0008](0008-employment-type-gates-the-practice-attachment-gates-the-engagement.md). Nothing switches on before the first hour is paid, and then `docs/research/ny-llc-tax-obligations.md`'s first-hire section is the list. The founder's reason, in his words: "yes"; the position rests on the reasoning put to him, that a minor should not be the person reading a Practice's Clients.

Weighed for: the record's own reason on #375; _Buy Back Your Time_'s interns and commission-only arrangements as transfers that cost nothing (ch. 2, PDF p. 57), corrected to a real wage since the exemption is on the tax and not the pay; _Start Small, Stay Small_'s graphic design and HTML outsourced every time (Ch. 1, pp. 24–25), as the kind of work it would be. Rejected: _Zero to One_'s everyone full-time (ch. 9, PDF p. 86), which cannot reach a child; _Make_'s and _Company of One_'s liability for another's income (Build, pp. 41–43; ch. 2, PDF p. 38), because a daughter's income is the household's already; a plan with a start date, because a date with no act behind it is the clock ADR-0043 refuses.

### The founder and the agents do everything, and help is a person paid for a named piece of work

The founder and the agents he runs do every question and every task, as far as their abilities allow; the wall has not been hit; what the pilot group and the customers know is the standing exception, which ADR-0045 P5 already holds as the market's evidence; and when the wall is hit, help is a person paid by contract for a named piece of work and never an employee. A human is paid for a named hour only when a ticket says so in its own body and says why being wrong there is expensive, as #383's §7 brief does and nothing else on the record does; the returns, the ST-100 and the IT-204-LL are filed by the founder with an agent, as [#385](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/385) and [#389](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/389) assume. Doula Cloud is nobody's mortgage. If a paid person ever acts inside the product she is a **platform operator**, the code's own word from migration 00055, where `granted_by` records the founder by name because the person issuing a founding grant is none of ADR-0022's three actor kinds; `CONTEXT.md` gains the term with this ADR. She is Staff, Owner, Admin or Doula at no Practice, is never displayed as "Doula Cloud", which ADR-0022 reserves for the product acting with nobody asking, reads nothing a Practice holds unless the piece of work names it, and inherits every refusal on the third position. If the work is ever regular, the ticket that seeks her names where she sits before it names her, because an employee working from another state carries physical presence with her and a first hire outside New York is a second sales-tax registration on day one (#375; `docs/research/ny-llc-tax-obligations.md`). This settles the synthesis's conflicts on the first hire, on Walling against Walling, on build versus outsource, on full-time staff or contracted help, and on titles: the Micropreneur side, build with agents, contracted, and no title, since a platform operator is an actor kind and not a rank. The synthesis's one agreement the record disagreed with, buy professional advice rather than employ it, is answered as advice bought by the named hour and never by retainer. The founder's reason, in his words: "yes, we do it all, as far as our abilities allow -- beyond that we will seek additional help, but haven't hit that wall yet (except obviously with matters that our pilot group needs to weigh in on, and stuff we'll learn from our customers)"; "re: outsourcing, you could say I _am_ outsourcing all of that wink wink"; and "sure" to the shape of help.

### The business is insured from the first real Client record

The business carries technology errors-and-omissions and cyber liability in one policy at the small-carrier tier, with the LLC as named insured, declines general liability, and binds the policy at the first pilot Practice's first real Client record. That is the day a claim becomes possible and not a day before; the LLC forms on 1 October 2026 ([#377](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/377)) and the pilot follows within days, so the policy is bought that week in the LLC's name from the LLC's account, from a carrier's own site with no broker, and the founder reads the professional-services definition and the bodily-injury exclusion himself before binding, under the eighth position's rule, recording the reading on #386; the BAA is re-executed in the LLC's name first (#546), so the named insured and the business associate are one entity. General liability is declined because there are no premises, no visitors and no product, and a Practice's contract is the only thing that would ever ask for it. Whether a pilot Practice requires proof of cover is asked on [#994](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/994)'s guide in the past tense, "has a software vendor ever been asked to show you a certificate of insurance", and the pilot terms on [#444](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/444) stay as they are. ## What was considered instead

**Full-time on Doula Cloud, or a trigger for leaving the employment.** Rejected because the employment carries the household's income and insurance and is what lets the business refuse outside money, and because a trigger on a date or a figure is a goal ADR-0046 refuses; whether he ever goes full-time is a question for after the first year's page exists.

**One person for now, growing past the ceiling; or the ADR-0048 bound left meaning nothing.** Rejected because the founder is not the moat and the feature list follows from the Engagement, so there is nothing a second person would be hired to grow, and a bound that means nothing is not a record.

**An assistant on the inbox, support outsourced, a standing auto-acknowledgment, or a published response time.** Rejected because the inbox is the promise ADR-0048 made, an auto-reply is the bot in front of her, and a number on `/support` is a promise measured by a clock that one person cannot keep on the night of a family birth.

**No bot at `hello@` at all.** The session's own recommendation, corrected by the founder: a program behind him that sorts and drafts and never speaks to her keeps the promise whole.

**A check that wakes him at any hour, no check, a standby database now, or a status page.** Rejected in the founder's own words on the first, and because a failure nobody knows about until a doula writes is a day lost, a standby is several times the database's cost against a $4,000 cap, and a status page counts nothing and is read by nobody at three in the morning.

**A cofounder, equity or profit share for anyone, outside money of any form, a professional CEO, or the three facts left as "before January".** Rejected because equity orients a person to a growth this company refuses, an investor's portfolio would ask every refusal in ADR-0048 to relax, a company of one has no handoff, and a rule reread on a date is ADR-0043's elapsed clock applied to the business.

**A named adviser role for anyone in the household, or nothing written.** Rejected because a title is a claim on the business the fifth position refuses; what the record keeps is that nobody in the household is named, with the founder's reason held off the record at his request.

**No cap on the founder's money, a cap with a consequence, or spending past it on judgment alone.** Rejected because a business with no ceiling on its owner's money has no answer to the unsustainable-costs vital sign, a consequence on a date is the clock ADR-0043 refuses, and a spend with no count beside it is a line on the monthly page nobody could read.

**The daughters as a plan with a start date, or as workers inside the product.** Rejected because a date with no act behind it is the clock ADR-0043 refuses, and a minor should not be the person reading a Practice's Clients.

**A lawyer on retainer and a standing accountant, a virtual assistant below an hourly rate, an agency or a designer, or an employee once the work is regular.** Rejected because a retainer buys hours nobody has asked a question for, the tasks a rate rule would send out are done by agents at no wage, the design and the copy are settled records, and a company of one that takes on a mortgage has stopped being one.

**Insurance bound at formation, "not yet" until the first purchased Credit, nothing before launch, general liability, or a broker.** Rejected because a week with no Client in the product buys nothing, the grant Credits put real families in from October, there are no premises and no product, and a one-person policy at this price is a form the founder reads himself.

## Consequences

- `docs/manifesto.md` gains one line for this ADR, and nothing else. `CONTEXT.md` gains one term, **Platform operator**, as the eighth position defines it, and nothing else changes.
- "In front of" and "behind" are the record's words for a program at `hello@`, sharpening ADR-0048 P2: a bot in front of her speaks to her and is refused; a bot behind the founder speaks only to him, may sort, label, route, summarize and draft, and may never send, answer, acknowledge, close, delay or drop a message. The founder reads `hello@` once a day and answers within a day; nothing publishes a time, and `/support` and the pilot terms keep "reaches a person" unchanged. An away notice is his own act, set and removed by him, naming only the day he is back.
- The product is built so that no night needs a person. [#1403](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1403) builds the two uptime checks, the phone channel and the waking-hours window in `terraform/`, turns point-in-time recovery on, and writes the single-zone database's accepted risk and its threshold beside the instance. No status page, no uptime figure, no Practice-facing message on an outage. The standby is bought the month the monthly page shows Credits bought covering it.
- One owner, standing: a ticket that proposes a cofounder, equity, an option, a profit share, an investor, or a C-corp is tested against this ADR, and a change lands as a superseding ADR, not an edit here. Nobody in the founder's household is named in the record.
- Every contact with DONA, the booth on [#1384](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1384) included, goes through DONA's published channels in the founder's own name, and nobody at DONA is contacted or copied personally. The reason is the founder's own and is not carried in this repository.
- The founder's money into the business is capped at $4,000 for 2027, everything included. Any spend past the cap, and any paid channel after launch, is a ticket that names the count of Credits bought it expects from the channel and the count read afterward on the monthly page. Every cost is paid from the LLC's account once it exists.
- A daughter paid for work is paid at a stated hourly rate with hours written down, for work that touches no Practice's data and no Client, and the first-hire section of `docs/research/ny-llc-tax-obligations.md` is read before the first hour is paid.
- A human is paid for a named hour only when a ticket says so in its own body and says why being wrong there is expensive. When the wall is hit, help is a person paid by contract for a named piece of work, never an employee; a ticket that seeks a regular person names where she sits before it names her; a person acting inside the product is a platform operator, recorded by name wherever a human decides, and inherits every refusal above.
- #386 carries the insurance decision: tech E&O and cyber in one policy, the LLC as named insured, general liability declined, bound at the first pilot Practice's first real Client record, after #546 re-executes the BAA in the LLC's name, with the definitions read by the founder before binding and the reading recorded there. #994's guide carries the past-tense certificate question, and #386 closes when its answer is recorded and the policy is bound.
- This is the eighth and last value ADR on the track opened by ADR-0041. Where a later session touches who works on Doula Cloud, who owns it, whose money is in it, what a program may do at `hello@`, or what a first hire changes, this ADR is the record it tests against.
