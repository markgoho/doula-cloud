# A Client record merges, and the Engagement moves with her

Two Client records at one Practice turn out to be the same woman, and both carry history. ADR-0017's amendment ([#727](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/727), built on [#814](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/814)) could absorb one record into another only while the absorbed one had nothing behind it — no Engagement, no Engagement Request, no portal access — because in that case nothing had to move. Where both records carried history the product refused, and said so on the screen. This decision reverses that refusal. Decided on [#813](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/813).

**Two rows become one, and everything that follows the woman moves to the survivor.** Her Engagements, her Engagement Requests and her accepted Portal Account links are re-pointed at the record that survives; the absorbed row keeps its id, gains `merged_into`, and is never written to again. Direction never depends on which record happens to be open for editing: the record carrying history survives, and where the two are alike — both attached, or neither — the older row survives.

The alternative considered and not chosen was a **recorded link**: both rows survive, neither is destroyed, and every screen shows the pair as one person. It reverses no recorded decision and is the smaller change. It was not chosen because Doula Cloud is pre-launch with no users and no production data, and a domain-model change of this size is cheap in September 2026 and expensive after the January 2027 launch. A link would have left every downstream read — Engagements, Invoices, Messages, the portal — resolving one woman through two ids forever, which is a cost paid on every screen rather than once in a migration. The third option, recording the deferral and building nothing, was declined for the same reason. No pilot Practice has yet reported this state, and the frequency is still unmeasured; the trade is being made deliberately anyway, because it gets more expensive by waiting rather than clearer.

**This supersedes "an Engagement belongs to one Client for life."** That sentence is ADR-0015's freeze rule and ADR-0017's own reading of it, and it is now false in one narrow, named case: a merge. Nothing else moves an Engagement, and the rule's original abuse case is untouched — serving a second baby on a Credit already spent is still what a free `client_id` would buy.

**The refusal is kept and narrowed rather than dropped, and it lives in the database.** "For life" was not only a sentence: `00093_engagement_birth_outcome.sql` carries an `engagements_freeze_outcome` BEFORE UPDATE trigger that raises `an Engagement belongs to one Client for life; client_id cannot be changed`. `00112` narrows it to admit a `client_id` change only where the old Client is already tombstoned into the new one — `EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM clients WHERE id = OLD.client_id AND merged_into = NEW.client_id)`. So the merge writes `merged_into` **before** it moves anything, and the database verifies a merge is under way from a durable row rather than from a session flag of the `app.allow_outcome_correction` kind. A session variable would let any writer that can set one move an Engagement anywhere; a tombstone can only be written by the merge endpoint, once, and it names exactly one destination.

**The two sealed histories stay two, permanently, and the screen says so.** ADR-0027 gives every Client her own 256-bit key in `client_data_keys` and seals every `subject_kind = 'client'` diff under it into `activity`, whose one refused trade (ADR-0022) is that a row is never updated or deleted. Re-sealing the absorbed woman's diffs under the survivor's key would rewrite rows that may never be rewritten. So **both keys survive the merge**, the merged Client carries two audit trails under one name, and the detail read labels every entry with the record it was written against rather than presenting one continuous history that never existed. An erasure of the merged Client shreds both keys and redacts both records.

**The merge's own audit is plaintext, because the sealed answer dies with the keys.** `clients.merged_at` and `clients.merged_by_staff_id` are columns, not a diff. The `merged` and `absorbed` activity rows are sealed under two client keys and an erasure destroys both, so a Practice asking "how did this record come to be?" afterwards would otherwise find `merged_into` pointing somewhere and no readable answer to who did it or when. This is the same rule the erasure scope already follows: describe the act, never a value that was erased.

**Two things cannot move, and neither is an omission.** `client_stripe_customers` carries no UPDATE grant at all — `00076` states plainly that repointing a Stripe Customer is not an operation this product has — so those rows stay on the absorbed record and are reached by erasure rather than by the merge. Her data key stays for the reason above. Erasure of the survivor therefore walks the `merged_into` chain and takes each absorbed record: redacted through a `SECURITY DEFINER` door, its Stripe Customers queued for deletion, its own key destroyed.

**Both Credits stay spent.** One Credit locks per Engagement and a locked Credit is never refundable. The Practice paid $20.00 twice and keeps having paid it. Nothing in a merge unlocks one, and nothing should: the work the second Credit paid for was real work that a Practice really did, whether or not the record it was booked against turned out to name the same woman.

**Two overlapping Engagements of the same kind need no new rule.** ADR-0017 already warns rather than refuses on a second concurrent Engagement, and the unique index blocking a second pending Engagement Request is keyed on kind rather than on Client, so a woman holding both a birth package and a postpartum package is already a permitted state. The one case that is refused is two *pending* Requests of one kind, one on each record: `engagement_requests_one_pending` would raise a constraint violation on the move, so the merge asks before it writes and refuses in words instead.

**A Client may now be reached by more than one Portal Account.** ADR-0015's rule was that a Portal Account "reaches many Clients, at most one per Practice", and that it is the only thing in the model that knows two Client records are the same woman. The first half is untouched — no login reaches two Clients at one Practice — but the reverse is now reachable: two logins, each accepted against one of two records before anybody noticed the two were one woman. And the second half is no longer true. A Staff-asserted `merged_into` knows it too, and it knows it from the Practice's side rather than from hers.

**An Engagement marked *This Engagement should never have existed* stops counting toward which record survives, and so does the approved Request that produced it.** It is not erased and it does not stay behind — it moves to the survivor like any other Engagement, because its Credit is still spent and the row still has to point somewhere. What changes is only the direction rule: a record holding nothing but a mistake loses to a record holding real care. Shipped with this decision because it is the answer to the likeliest duplicate — one pregnancy typed twice, and each record given an Engagement before anyone noticed. Discounting the Engagement alone would have been a no-op: an Engagement exists only because an Engagement Request was approved, so the Request would have kept the record attached on its own. A refused or withdrawn Request still counts, because a decision not to take somebody on is real history of a real conversation rather than a typo.

**How often this happens is still unknown.** No pilot Practice has reported two Client records for one woman, and neither route — one pregnancy typed twice, or the same woman entered fresh years later — has been ruled out. The design covers both. That the frequency is unmeasured is recorded here rather than treated as a reason to wait.
