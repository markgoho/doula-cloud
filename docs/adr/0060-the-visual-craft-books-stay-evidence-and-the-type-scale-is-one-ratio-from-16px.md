# The visual craft books stay evidence, and the type scale is one 1.25 ratio from a 16px floor

DoulaCloud's look is decided on record. [The brief](../design/brief.md), the *Plum Dusk, evolved* direction chosen on [#409](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/409) from four rendered directions and amended on [#417](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/417) and [#433](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/433), sets one family, eight type steps picked by hand (12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 20, 28 and 36px), three weights, a 4px spacing base, one accent, no shadow and one radius. `app/src/lib/styles/tokens.css` holds those values, and [#531](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/531) made every size and space a fluid step on one ramp, with one growth factor per scale. #531 declined a modular ratio: "Reopening the ratio is a ticket against #417, not this one." [ADR-0021](0021-govuk-is-the-reference-for-service-patterns.md) makes `tokens.css` "the color, type and spacing truth" and says "GOV.UK never overrules a token". Under [ADR-0041](0041-a-book-enters-the-repo-as-evidence-and-becomes-a-rule-only-through-a-decision.md), a book is evidence and only the founder's recorded decision turns it into a rule. Two books on the reference track that are wholly or mostly on this theme, *Refactoring UI* and *Flexible Typesetting*, had no decision for it. [ADR-0055](0055-form-design-patterns-is-a-rank-three-source-for-forms.md) left the look in *Designing User Interfaces* to this theme, and ADR-0055 and [ADR-0057](0057-inclusive-components-stays-a-rank-three-source-and-a-control-that-acts-at-once-is-a-switch.md) declined *Inclusive Design Patterns* for forms and components without its type chapter.

On 2026-10-24 the founder worked through the theme synthesis at [`docs/research/books/themes/visual-craft-and-type.md`](../research/books/themes/visual-craft-and-type.md) in the visual craft and type grilling session, [#1722](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1722). Round 1 had seven questions, each with a recommended answer ([round 1](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1722#issuecomment-6392273380), [answers](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1722#issuecomment-6392405677)). He took the recommendation on five of them. On how the type scale is picked he asked for a prototype, and on the size of body text he chose 16px everywhere, not the recommendation. The prototype, [#1926](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1926), rendered six candidate scales on real screens, and he picked one on the rendered page ([round 2](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1722#issuecomment-6393075110)). He confirmed the positions record of seven positions the same day ("Confirmed"), with its two open points settled ([positions record](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1722#issuecomment-6393076068), [confirmation](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1722#issuecomment-6393179432)). This ADR records those decisions. On the type scale the reason is the founder's, in his own words; on body text it is his answer as round 1 records it; on the four books, on growth per step and on all-caps text he took the recommendation, and the reason is the recommendation's, from round 1 on #1722. A book appears only as evidence weighed.

**No book is adopted. *Refactoring UI*, *Flexible Typesetting*, *Designing User Interfaces* and *Inclusive Design Patterns* stay evidence for this theme, with no rank, and the eight other books on the track keep the standing earlier ADRs gave them. The type scale changes: one ratio, 1.25, from a 16px floor, so body text is 16px everywhere and nothing is under 16px; one growth factor, 1.2, still holds for every step; and no text is set in all caps. Color, spacing, depth and shape do not change. The product changes, and each change is carried by its own ticket: [#1943](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1943), [#1944](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1944), [#1945](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1945) and [#1946](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1946). With this ADR, each of the twelve books on the reference track has an adopt-or-not decision.**

## The decision

### *Refactoring UI* stays evidence, with no rank

*Refactoring UI* (Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger, 2018; [read-back](../research/books/refactoring-ui.md)) is not a source. It takes no place in ADR-0021's order of sources (GOV.UK, then NHS.UK and ONS, then *Inclusive Components* and Adrian Roselli's writing (and, for forms, *Form Design Patterns*), then Every Layout, then original work with a recorded reason), and no place beside the brief. Where a position below weighs one of its claims, the claim and its page are named as evidence, not the book as a source. [ADR-0058](0058-atomic-design-names-the-tiers-and-two-consumers-that-should-change-together-earn-a-variant.md) already kept what the book says about a system as evidence; this position covers its values.

The founder took the recommendation (#1722, Q1 and position 1), so the reason is round 1's: the brief already holds every value the book would give, and where the two differ the brief chose by looking at four rendered directions on #409, which is better evidence than the book's own figures. A visual question the brief does not answer goes to the brief by ticket, as its "This is not a license to add a fifth" asks, so a second visual reference would be a second place to look for one answer.

Weighed for: two weights for most text (Hierarchy is Everything, p. 34); a type scale picked by hand (Designing Text, pp. 89–91); 45 to 75 characters a line (pp. 99–100); no true black and grays with a hue (Working with Color, pp. 125, 139–141); shades defined up front (p. 129); and a flat design where a lighter surface reads as closer (Creating Depth, p. 168).

Weighed against: "It's not a science" (Working with Color, p. 132); the 25% gap, the 45 to 75 characters and the shade counts carry no source, and the book never names Tailwind CSS (read-back section 8); its HSL rests on a browser fact that is no longer true (read-back section 7); and five shadow elevations (Creating Depth, pp. 151–161) and nine shades a color (Working with Color, pp. 125–131) are what #409 did not choose.

Rejected: adopting it as the reference for a visual question the brief does not answer, below the brief and `tokens.css`, because that is a second place to look; and adopting it with its values over the brief's, because ADR-0021 lets no source overrule a token.

### *Flexible Typesetting* stays evidence, with no rank

*Flexible Typesetting* (Tim Brown, 2018; [read-back](../research/books/flexible-typesetting.md)) is not a source, and takes no place in ADR-0021's order or beside the brief.

The founder took the recommendation (#1722, Q2 and position 2), so the reason is round 1's: what the book asks that a gate can check, the root left to the reader and sizes in `rem`, the repo already does and `zoom.svelte.spec.ts` proves. What it asks that would change the brief is its method, a scale from a ratio and a body size from an anecdotal measure, which is the part its own read-back finds weakest. Position 4 below now uses a ratio above body, as Brown does (Ch. 4, pp. 99–101), but the reason for it is the founder's pick on the rendered page, not the book.

Weighed for: the reader's default size is "a rock" and the root is 100% (Ch. 2, pp. 40–43); `rem` or `em` with a viewport unit in `calc()` is accessible, and pixels or viewport units alone are not (pp. 44–46); size, then measure, then line spacing (Ch. 4, p. 91); measure "is a visual decision" (p. 103); and links are underlined (p. 136).

Weighed against: no "correct" sizes, measures or line heights (Ch. 1, p. 13); the familiar body size from measurements the author calls "anecdotal" (Ch. 4, pp. 93–98); one small site, and an unreconciled warning against growing body text (read-back section 8); and the container queries it hoped for have shipped (read-back section 7).

Rejected: adopting it for type only, below the brief.

### *Designing User Interfaces* and *Inclusive Design Patterns* stay evidence for this theme

*Designing User Interfaces* (Michal and Diana Malewicz, 2020; [read-back](../research/books/designing-user-interfaces.md)) and *Inclusive Design Patterns* (Heydon Pickering, 2016; [read-back](../research/books/inclusive-design-patterns.md)) are not sources for the look. This finishes what ADR-0055 left to this theme for the Malewiczes' look, and adds this theme to the ones ADR-0055 and ADR-0057 declined *Inclusive Design Patterns* for.

The founder took the recommendation (#1722, Q3 and position 3), so the reason is round 1's: each point where the two books agree with the brief the brief already holds, and each point where they differ (shadows, a 1.618 line height, a 16px body) is either refused by the brief or taken up in position 5 through *Form Design Patterns*, the rank-3 source for forms.

Weighed for the Malewiczes: an 8-point grid (Layout and grid, pp. 61–64); 16 to 18 points for body text and form fields on mobile (Typography, p. 159); 3 to 5 sizes and two weights (p. 165); no pure black (Colors, pp. 107, 122–123); and the call to action as the only uniquely colored element (Buttons, p. 186). Weighed against: their numbers are their studio's practice with uncited figures (read-back section 8); and shadows at up to 40% (Objects, pp. 88–90) and a line height of 1.618 times the size (Typography, p. 144) are the opposite of the brief.

Weighed for *Inclusive Design Patterns*: the root at 100% (The Document, p. 29); a 45 to 75 character measure (A Paragraph, pp. 52–53); leading of 1.5 (p. 55); tempered contrast (p. 57); and "an `<h2>` should look like an `<h2>`" (A Blog Post, p. 79). Weighed against: its evidence is the author's experience and informal polls (read-back section 8), and the same author's later book holds rank 3 (ADR-0057).

Rejected: adopting either one for the look, below the brief.

### The other books on the track

The other eight books keep the standing an earlier ADR gave them, and this ADR adds no rank for any of them. *Form Design Patterns* is a rank-3 source for forms by ADR-0055; *Inclusive Components* is at rank 3 by ADR-0057; *Giving a Damn About Accessibility* is declined as a source by ADR-0057; *Atomic Design* is adopted for the tier names and the template–page split only by ADR-0058, and *Design Systems*, *Expressive Design Systems* and *Laying the Foundations* stay evidence by the same ADR (its position 2); and *Going Offline* is adopted for its principles only by [ADR-0059](0059-going-offline-is-adopted-for-its-principles-and-the-app-is-offline-maybe-by-launch.md), and has no position on this theme, as the synthesis records. The only color it names, the web app manifest's (Ch. 9, p. 124), `tokens.spec.ts` already holds to `--color-primary` and `--color-surface` ([#1486](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1486)). Where a position below weighs a claim from one of them, the claim and its page are named as evidence. *Atomic Design*'s 20-second gut test, style tiles and element collages (ch. 4, pp. 126–130) are a way to find values, and the values were found on #409 by rendering four directions. *Giving a Damn About Accessibility*'s contrast on more than text (Ch. 4, pp. 41–42) is ADR-0057's AA gate.

### The type scale is one ratio, 1.25, from a 16px floor up, and nothing is under 16px

The eight steps keep their names and take new floors, at a 320px column:

| Step | Floor | Was | Ratio to the step below |
|---|---|---|---|
| `display` | 39.06px | 36px | 1.25 |
| `heading-lg` | 31.25px | 28px | 1.25 |
| `heading` | 25px | 20px | 1.25 |
| `subheading` | 20px | 16px | 1.25 |
| `body` | 16px | 15px | 1.0 |
| `body-sm` | 16px | 14px | 1.0 |
| `label` | 16px | 13px | 1.0 |
| `meta` | 16px | 12px | — |

The four roles from `body` down are one size, and they differ only by weight (`label` 500, the others 400) and by the muted color where a component asks for it. Each keeps its own line height (`meta` 1.4, `label` 1.35, `body-sm` 1.5, `body` 1.55) and tracking (`meta` `0.01em`, `label` `0.005em`, the others `0`) as `tokens.css` has them today, which is what the prototype rendered. The weights, line heights and tracking of the four heading steps do not change either. This replaces #531's hand-picked floors. #531's ramp, its 1.2 growth factor (position 6), its "Nothing in the app gets smaller than it is today" and its four fixed values hold, and every new floor is at or above the old one.

The founder's words, in order (#1722, round 2 and position 4): "let's go with 4 of 5, 1.25 ratio"; then, asked what happens below body, "I feel like we need to have a "smallest" font of 16px and go up from there, can we do that?"; then, after he saw scale 6 with the wrapping measured in the prototype's Clients list in its wide column, "Option 6, fix tables". On how line height and tracking are kept below body, he took the recommendation in the confirmation: hold each role's own values as the prototype rendered them, because that is what he chose from.

Weighed for a ratio: Brown builds the scale from the body size and a ratio, because related numbers "feel harmonious" (*Flexible Typesetting*, Ch. 4, pp. 99–101); and Perez-Cruz sets a base and a ratio, a higher ratio for more contrast (*Expressive Design Systems*, Ch. 3, pp. 66–67; Ch. 4, p. 93). Weighed for few sizes: most interfaces use "way too many font sizes" (*Refactoring UI*, Designing Text, p. 88); the Malewiczes' 3 to 5 sizes (Typography, p. 165), and this scale has five; and Couldwell's "a few header styles, and a few copy styles" (*Laying the Foundations*, Chapter 8, p. 157). Weighed for hierarchy below body by weight and color, not size: *Refactoring UI*'s weight and color "usually enough" (Hierarchy is Everything, pp. 32–34).

Weighed against: *Refactoring UI*'s modular scale gives fractional pixels and jumps "too limiting" for interface work (Designing Text, pp. 89–91), and 31.25 and 39.06 are fractional; the Malewiczes keep smaller sizes for descriptions, labels and metadata (Typography, p. 159); *Refactoring UI*'s scale runs down to 12px (Designing Text, p. 91); and Brown allows a reader to "eyeball" a scale (Ch. 4, p. 102) and says there is no single "correct" way (Ch. 5, p. 150).

Rejected, the round 1 recommendation and the other five prototype scales (#1926):

- **Hold the hand-picked steps** (the round 1 recommendation), whose reason was that dense tables and labels need small sizes close together. The founder's words on it in round 1: "ooh this isn't great is it -- I thought we'd have some nice ratio, but we don't". His later "smallest font of 16px" takes away the small sizes that reason rested on, and "fix tables" moves the cost to the table's own words (#1945).
- **Scale 1, hand-picked, fixed** (12, 13, 14, 16, 18, 20, 28 and 36px): not a ratio, which his round 1 words asked for, and three steps under 16px.
- **Scale 2, one ratio of 1.125** (`meta` 11.24px to `display` 25.63px), and **scale 3, one ratio of 1.2** (9.26 to 33.18px, with `body-sm` at 13.33px): not picked; he named 1.25 from the five and gave no other reason.
- **Scale 4, one ratio of 1.25 from body down as well as up** (8.19 to 39.06px): his first pick, kept above body and replaced below it, because `meta` 8.19, `label` 10.24 and `body-sm` 12.8px are under the 16px "smallest" font he then asked for.
- **Scale 5, two ratios**, 1.067 below body and 1.25 above (13.17 to 39.06px): the same steps above body as scale 6, but three steps under 16px.

A correction to the record: #531's "this repo's six sizes sit at roughly a **1.067** ratio" was wrong. The brief had eight steps and no single ratio: the five small steps sat 7% to 8% apart and the three heading steps 25% to 40% apart (#1722, round 1, Q4). A dated note on #531 says so (#1944).

### Body text is 16px everywhere

`body` is 16px, and so is every step below it (position 4), so a form field's text, prose, list rows and table cells are all at 16px or more. A separate field token is not needed. It stops Safari on iPhone from zooming the page in when a person taps a field whose text is under 16px, which is a browser fact, not a book claim, and the kind of shift the brief's smoothness list ("Nothing shifts after it paints") rules out. With `body-sm` at 16px, the brief's "compact rows" comes from the 40px row and the spacing, not from a smaller size.

The founder chose this in round 1 (#1722, Q5 and position 5) over the recommendation, a 16px floor in a field only. Round 1 records his answer, "body text is 16px everywhere", and no words of his own for it; his round 2 words, "a "smallest" font of 16px and go up from there", carry it to every step below body.

Weighed for: Silver's "probably means a font size of at least 16 pixels" (*Form Design Patterns*, rank 3 for forms, Ch. 1, p. 32); the Malewiczes' 16 to 18 points for body text and form fields on mobile (Typography, p. 159); and *Inclusive Design Patterns*' 16px default with "a rationale" (The Document, p. 29). Weighed against: Perez-Cruz's never under 14px (Ch. 3, p. 66); *Refactoring UI*'s scale down to 12px (Designing Text, p. 91); and Brown's familiar size of 1.15rem (Ch. 4, pp. 93–98).

Rejected: holding 15px; and the recommendation, a 16px floor in a field with prose and rows kept at 15px and 14px, whose reason was the brief's "compact rows".

### One growth factor, 1.2, for every type step

Every type step still grows from its floor to a ceiling of its floor times 1.2 on the ramp, as #531 set it and as [`GLOSSARY.md`](../../GLOSSARY.md)'s **Growth factor** defines it, "chosen once for a whole scale rather than per step". So the gap between a heading and body text is the same in a narrow column as in a wide one.

The founder took the recommendation (#1722, Q6 and position 6), so the reason is round 1's: the floors are the brief's sizes and "Nothing in the app gets smaller than it is today", so the books' advice could only be taken by making headings larger on a wide screen, which spends that room on letters against **The ramp**'s "more room buys more content, not larger letters". One factor is also one number `tokens.spec.ts` checks for the whole scale. Position 4's new floors keep both: every new floor is at or above the old one, and each ceiling is its floor times 1.2.

Weighed for: *Inclusive Design Patterns* grows every step together from one root rule (The Document, pp. 31–32); and Brown warns that body text that changes as a layout widens moves readers away from their familiar size (Ch. 5, p. 146). Weighed against: Brown's less contrast between steps as a layout narrows (Ch. 5, pp. 164–166); and *Refactoring UI*'s large elements that "need to shrink faster" (Layout and Spacing, pp. 79–81). Silver (*Form Design Patterns*, Ch. 7, p. 308) and Perez-Cruz (Ch. 4, p. 95) grow size or space on a large screen, but say not by how much per step.

Rejected: a larger factor for the heading steps than for the text steps.

### No all-caps text

No text is set in capitals by CSS. The two group labels that are today, "Your practices" over the Practice list in `molecules/PracticeSwitcher.svelte` and "Practice" over the switcher in the narrow sheet of `organisms/StaffTopBar.svelte`, each with `text-transform: uppercase`, are set in sentence case, as their words are already written in the source. The brief gains the rule.

The founder took the recommendation (#1722, Q7 and position 7), so the reason is round 1's: the brief makes hierarchy from "size, weight and tracking", and `meta` in `on-surface-muted` already marks a group label. Capitals are a second signal that would need a tracking token of their own to be set well, and one rule with no exception is fewer parts than a token for two labels.

Weighed for a looser tracking token: *Refactoring UI* loosens all-caps text (Designing Text, pp. 115–117); and Brown loosens small text (Ch. 5, pp. 152, 164). Weighed against: `tokens.usage.spec.ts` allows no tracking outside the scale, so either claim would add a token.

Rejected: holding capitals at `meta`'s `0.01em`; and a looser tracking token for all-caps text.

### Where the other conflicts rest

The synthesis names sixteen conflicts. Four are decided above: how a type scale is picked (position 4), how large body text is (position 5), whether type grows with its room and keeps its ratio (position 6), and the evidence each book rests on (the ground of positions 1 to 3). Position 7 decides all-caps text, a question beside the letter-spacing conflict, which holds below. The other twelve hold on records that predate this ADR, as round 1 on #1722 sets out:

- **What sets line height.** The brief, per step, falling as size grows (1.15 at `display` to 1.55 at `body`), which is *Refactoring UI*'s rule that line height falls as size grows (Designing Text, pp. 105–108); running text meets *Inclusive Design Patterns*' 1.5 (A Paragraph, p. 55). Line height that rises with line length (*Refactoring UI*, same pages; Brown, Ch. 4, pp. 106–109) has nothing to act on, because prose is capped at `--measure`; Brown's own example keeps 1.5 (p. 110). The Malewiczes' 1.618 (Typography, p. 144) and Perez-Cruz's one global value (Ch. 3, pp. 66–67) come from books that are not sources.
- **How a measure is found.** `--measure: 65ch` ([#98](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/98)), the brief's "Prose gets a measure", and [#609](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/609)'s cap on a form's prose. 65 characters sits inside the 45 to 75 of *Refactoring UI* (Designing Text, pp. 99–100) and *Inclusive Design Patterns* (A Paragraph, pp. 52–53), counted in characters as *Inclusive Components* asks (Modal Dialogs, p. 281). Brown's "a visual decision" has a count as its check (Ch. 4, p. 103; Ch. 5, p. 149), and the count is what a token can hold.
- **The unit a size is written in.** `GLOSSARY.md`'s **Fluid step**, a `rem` floor and ceiling with a `cqi` growth term, "so a person's own font-size setting still moves it (WCAG 1.4.4)"; `html` sets no font size (`base.css`), as *Inclusive Design Patterns* and Brown ask (The Document, p. 29; Ch. 2, pp. 40–43); and `tokens.usage.spec.ts` refuses a raw `px` length in a component. *Inclusive Design Patterns*' heading margin in `em` (A Blog Post, p. 103) has nothing to attach to, because a layout primitive spaces its children with `gap` ([ADR-0039](0039-a-layout-primitive-spaces-its-children-with-gap.md)).
- **The base of the spacing scale.** The brief's "The spacing scale stays on a 4px base", from the [#406](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/406) survey, and `tokens.css`'s ten steps. Perez-Cruz's 4px (Ch. 3, p. 68) and Couldwell's added 4px step (Chapter 8, p. 175) agree, and the Malewiczes' 8 (Layout and grid, pp. 61–64) is two of its steps. *Refactoring UI*'s 25% gap (Layout and Spacing, pp. 61–63) is there so a person without a designer can choose between two near values; the brief names each near step by its use.
- **How many steps a color gets.** The brief and #417, a role per need and no numbered shades, which is Kholmatova's palette of only what its roles need (*Design Systems*, ch. 9, pp. 227–229). *Refactoring UI*'s nine shades (Working with Color, pp. 125–131) and Couldwell's seven (Chapter 8, pp. 154–155) answer a drift that `tokens.usage.spec.ts` stops ("names no color of its own"). `Badge`'s tints are mixed in `oklab` ([ADR-0019](0019-pen-dev-is-the-working-surface-and-code-is-the-truth.md), [#434](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/434), [#659](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/659)).
- **The color model.** OKLCH, for `tokens.css`'s own reason ("a perceptual lightness axis is what makes the dark theme a derivation"), with `tokens.spec.ts` refusing any other. *Refactoring UI*'s HSL rests on browsers that "only understand HSL" (Working with Color, pp. 119–122), no longer true (read-back section 7).
- **Depth.** The brief's "No drop shadows anywhere", with depth from the surface ladder and one-pixel borders (#409), which is *Refactoring UI*'s flat design (Creating Depth, p. 168) with fewer borders (Finishing Touches, pp. 206–209), and Kholmatova's "1px light-gray strokes" (ch. 4, p. 105). *Refactoring UI*'s five elevations (pp. 151–161) and the Malewiczes' shadows (Objects, pp. 88–90; Buttons, p. 194) are the option #409 did not choose.
- **Corner radius.** The brief's one 8px radius. *Refactoring UI*'s small radius reads as neutral (Starting from Scratch, pp. 21–22), and the Malewiczes take the radius from the grid base (Objects, p. 84); 8px is two steps of the 4px base.
- **Whether a heading looks like its level.** #417's "Two axes, two owners" in `Heading.svelte`: `level` is the structure and `variant` the size, *Refactoring UI*'s rule (Hierarchy is Everything, pp. 46–47), and the default variant follows the level, *Inclusive Design Patterns*' caution (A Blog Post, p. 79).
- **How a link looks.** `atoms/Link.svelte`, an underline at rest, as Brown and *Inclusive Design Patterns* ask (Ch. 4, p. 136; A Paragraph, pp. 58–60). *Refactoring UI*'s underline "only on hover" (Designing Text, pp. 109–110) is for an interface where almost everything is a link; this app draws those links as controls, each a variant of its own.
- **Letter-spacing.** The brief's "Tracking tightens as size grows and loosens as it shrinks", one value per step, which `tokens.usage.spec.ts` holds. *Refactoring UI*'s tighter headlines (Designing Text, pp. 115–117), Brown's looser small text (Ch. 5, pp. 152, 164) and Couldwell's documented value per style (Chapter 9, p. 198) are each met. Below body, where position 4 puts four roles at one size, each keeps its own tracking.
- **A dark theme.** The brief's "Derive, do not invent", following the system setting with a `[data-theme]` override that only `/style-guide` sets, which meets *Inclusive Components*' low-light theme (A Theme Switcher, p. 116) and its system choice honored by default (p. 128). The Malewiczes' "purely an aesthetic choice" (Design Styles, p. 347) is from a book that is not a source.

The other standing records the round named also hold: three weights, 400, 500 and 600 (*Refactoring UI*'s two weights for most text and one for emphasis, Hierarchy is Everything, p. 34; Couldwell's "Pick one", Chapter 9, p. 196); what every book agrees with, from one family to tabular figures and "compact rows, airy forms"; `fonts.css`'s metric-compatible fallback, [ADR-0024](0024-layout-is-intrinsic-and-320px-is-a-conformance-commitment.md)'s rule 4 and the gates; and the marketing site and `brand.md`, through the brief's "Adopting this in `site/`", [#1000](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1000), [ADR-0050](0050-the-product-is-named-doulacloud-one-word.md) and [ADR-0051](0051-the-marketing-site-is-sveltekit-not-hugo.md).

### What the books add to standing decisions, and what they change

The color and spacing are unchanged. The type changes in positions 4, 5 and 7, and those changes come from the founder's picks on the rendered prototype, with the books weighed as evidence and none adopted.

- **The brief.** Changes: its type table takes position 4's floors, with weights, line heights and tracking as `tokens.css` has them; its "compact rows" line ("A table row is `40px` at `body-sm`") is written for a 16px `body-sm`; its sentence that Hanken Grotesk "stays legible at 13–14px in a dense table" has no 13 or 14px size left to describe; and its tracking rule says that below body each role keeps its own tracking at one size (#1944). It gains the rule "no all-caps text" (#1946). Holds: its Color, Density spacing values, Depth and shape, and "Adopting this in `site/`".

## What was considered instead

**Adopt nothing, and change nothing.** A legitimate outcome under [#1684](https://github.com/markgoho/doula-cloud/issues/1684). Taken for the books: none is adopted (positions 1 to 3). Not taken for the product: the founder moved the type scale (positions 4 and 5) and set a rule for capitals (position 7) on his own picks.

**Adopt *Refactoring UI* as the visual reference below the brief.** Rejected under position 1, because it would be a second place to look for one answer.

**Hold the hand-picked steps.** Round 1's recommendation on Q4, rejected under position 4, in the founder's words: "ooh this isn't great is it -- I thought we'd have some nice ratio, but we don't".

**A 16px floor for the text in a field only.** Round 1's recommendation on Q5, rejected under position 5, because the founder chose 16px for body text everywhere.

**One ratio of 1.25 all the way down.** Scale 4, his first pick, replaced below body under position 4, because it puts three steps under the 16px "smallest" font he asked for.

**A larger growth factor for the heading steps.** Rejected under position 6, because it spends a wide column's room on larger letters.

**A looser tracking token for all-caps text.** Rejected under position 7, because one rule with no exception is fewer parts than a token for two labels.
